Three ways agencies price, and what each does to behaviour

Whatever the number turns out to be, it will be built on one of these three. Understanding the incentive is more useful than the digit.

  • Flat retainer. Predictable, easy to budget, and indifferent to your spend. The risk is that a fixed fee against a growing account gets quietly under-serviced, and against a shrinking one feels punitive.
  • Percentage of media spend. The fee moves with the account, which ties the agency to growth. reMKTR charges this way, so we will not pretend it is the wrong model — but it needs two safeguards written into the contract: a rate that steps down as spend rises, and a review trigger if spend grows faster than results.
  • Hybrid. A base plus a percentage. The most common enterprise structure, and the one where costs are easiest to lose track of, because the two components are usually negotiated in separate conversations.

Add onboarding or setup fees, which are common at enterprise scale, and make sure you know whether they are one-time or repeat on scope changes.

What drives the quote

Five inputs decide where an enterprise proposal lands, and you can shape all five before you get one.

  • Media under management. The first question they will ask, and the biggest lever on price.
  • Channel count. Amazon alone is one scope. Amazon plus Google, Meta, CTV and programmatic is a different business relationship and a different number.
  • Creative. Display and video production is real work and is frequently a separate line. Ask whether it is in or out.
  • Catalogue complexity. Hundreds of ASINs across variations and marketplaces takes more hours than a tight range does.
  • Measurement scope. Amazon Marketing Cloud work, incrementality testing and custom reporting are often priced apart from campaign management.

Get each of those confirmed in the proposal rather than in the meeting. Scope written down is what you can hold people to at renewal.

The question nobody raises on the call: are you a big account here?

Tinuiti is one of the largest independent performance marketing agencies in the United States. That size is a genuine advantage — bench depth, platform relationships, people who have seen your problem at scale, and the ability to staff a launch quickly.

It also means their team structure is built around large budgets. If your Amazon spend would place you in the bottom quartile of their book, you are likely to be staffed accordingly, and the senior people you meet in the pitch are unlikely to be the ones in your account weekly. That is not a criticism, it is arithmetic that applies to every large agency including the ones we admire.

So ask it directly: where does our budget sit in your client base, who specifically works on this account, how many other accounts do they hold, and what changes if our spend halves? A good agency answers all four without flinching.

Common questions

How much does Tinuiti charge?

They do not say publicly, and the figures circulating in blog roundups are not sourced from Tinuiti. Ask for a written proposal naming the fee basis, whether creative and Amazon Marketing Cloud work are included, and what the rate does as spend grows.

Does Tinuiti have a minimum spend?

No minimum is published. Large agencies generally set one commercially rather than publicly. Ask directly, and ask the follow-up that matters more: where your budget would sit in their client base and who would be assigned to it.

Is a percentage of ad spend a fair way to pay an agency?

Yes, when it is structured properly. It ties the fee to the account rather than to hours. Ask for a rate that steps down as spend rises and a review trigger if spend outpaces results, so growth in the fee has to be earned.

Do I need an agency for Amazon DSP at all?

Not necessarily. Amazon's own product page describes a typical minimum investment of USD 50,000 for its managed-service option, and self-service exists if you have a trader. Agencies holding seats are the usual middle path, with more flexible minimums and someone accountable weekly.

How should I compare agency proposals fairly?

Normalise them. Put total annual cost, what is included, who is assigned, and how results will be proved into the same four columns. Then ask each one the same measurement question and compare the answers, not the decks.
