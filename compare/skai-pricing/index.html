Skai Pricing: What Is Actually Published, and What It Buys

Skai publishes its pricing, which is rare in this category. On skai.io/pricing right now: Standard at $114k per year up to $4M in annual spend, Advanced $276k up to $10M, Enterprise $504k up to $20M, Enterprise Premier $756k up to $35M, all USD. Above $35M is quoted.

Skai posts a number. Almost nobody else in this category does

Start with the thing that should count in Skai's favour. Their pricing page carries named tiers with dollar figures attached. We checked it directly rather than trusting a roundup, and the tiers were there: Standard, Advanced, Enterprise, Enterprise Premier, and a quoted band above that.

Compare that with the rest of the shelf. Pacvue's pricing URL returns a 404. Criteo has no pricing link in its navigation at all. Tinuiti and Flywheel publish nothing. In a category where "contact sales" is the default answer, printing a ladder you can read before the call is a real difference, and it changes how the negotiation starts.

It also means the numbers on this page can be checked. Everything in the answer above came off Skai's own page, in US dollars, with the tier names as they print them. Check their current pricing before you budget from it — vendors move.

The ladder indexes spend, and that is where the real cost hides

Each tier is capped by annual media spend, not by seats or features alone. Standard covers up to $4M. Advanced up to $10M. Enterprise up to $20M. Enterprise Premier up to $35M.

Run the arithmetic on the ceiling of each band and the fee looks reasonable: $114k against $4M is about 2.9% of media, and $756k against $35M is about 2.2%. Fees usually taper as spend grows, and Skai's does.

Now run it at the floor of a band, which is where most buyers actually sit. A brand spending $1.5M a year on the Standard tier is paying $114k, which is 7.6% of media. A brand at $5M just crossed into Advanced and pays $276k, or 5.5%. The band you land in matters more than the headline rate, and the worst place to sit is one dollar over a threshold.

That is the single most useful question to take into a Skai call: not "what does it cost", but "what happens to my fee at the spend I will actually be at in eighteen months".

What the fee does not include

Software pricing is only ever part of the bill. Three things sit outside it:

  • The media itself. Every dollar of Amazon DSP, sponsored ads, search and social budget is on top.
  • Creative. Display and video units still have to be made, sized, versioned and refreshed. That is a separate line, whether it is an in-house designer or an outside studio.
  • The operator. This is the one people underestimate. A cross-channel platform gives an experienced trader more leverage. It does not supply the trader. If nobody on your team owns the account daily, the platform will faithfully report a problem nobody fixes.

Skai's own tiers acknowledge this — client success and onboarding scale up the ladder, and incrementality testing appears at the Premier level rather than at the bottom.

Who should buy Skai anyway

Plenty of readers should, and we would rather say so than pretend otherwise.

Skai is a serious enterprise platform with real breadth — retail media, paid search and paid social in one login, 300+ publisher integrations, and a measurement layer built to compare channels against each other. If your Amazon spend is one slice of a much larger media budget, and the executive question in the room is "how does Amazon compare to Google and Meta this quarter", a single-channel managed service does not answer that. Skai does.

It also suits organisations that already employ traders. If you have a team who will be in the platform every day, buying software rather than service is the cheaper and more controllable choice. The tier ladder is then a straightforward budgeting exercise.

Where it fits badly: a brand whose whole business is Amazon, spending under a few million a year, with nobody dedicated to running the account. At that size the effective fee percentage is at its worst and the breadth you are paying for goes unused.

Common questions

How much does Skai cost per year?

As published on Skai's own pricing page: Standard $114k, Advanced $276k, Enterprise $504k and Enterprise Premier $756k per year, all USD, each capped by an annual media spend band. Above $35M in spend is quoted individually. Confirm against their current page before budgeting.

Is there a monthly Skai plan?

Pricing is presented annually, and the page describes predictable annual pricing with the flexibility to review the commitment after the first three months. Treat the exact commitment language as something to confirm in writing during contracting rather than something to assume.

Does the Skai fee include my ad spend?

No. The tier price is for the platform. Media, creative production and the salaried people running campaigns are all separate. When you compare a platform fee to an agency percentage, make sure you are counting the same things on both sides.

Can Skai run Amazon DSP?

Skai supports Amazon DSP among its retail media channels, and its own case studies reference it. The practical question is not integration but operation — a platform that can reach DSP still needs someone building audiences, rotating creative and reading incrementality every week.

What is a fair way to compare Skai against a managed DSP service?

Put total cost of ownership side by side: platform fee, plus creative, plus the loaded salary of whoever operates it, against a single managed percentage. Then compare what each side can prove about incremental sales, not just what each reports as attributed.
