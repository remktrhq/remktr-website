What is actually true today

Three facts, each opened at source rather than taken from a summary.

  • The rename happened in June 2021. The company trades as Skai and the product is positioned as a commerce media platform covering retail media, paid search and paid social.
  • The corporate entity did not change. The footer on skai.io reads "© 2026 Kenshoo, Ltd. All Rights Reserved" — which is precisely why the old name refuses to die in search results, and why plenty of people reasonably assume they are two companies.
  • The old name's review listing is empty. On G2, read 21 August 2026, the entry titled "Kenshoo Infinity Suite" carries no rating and no reviews. The live corpus sits under the Skai listing at 4.0 out of 5 across 297 reviews.

That last pair is the whole problem in miniature. A buyer searching the name they remember lands on a listing with nothing in it, concludes the product is obscure or abandoned, and never reaches the 297-review record two clicks away. The opposite error is just as common: finding an old article that quotes Kenshoo capabilities and pricing, and budgeting against a product that has since been rebuilt.

How a stale name gets onto a shortlist in the first place

This is not carelessness. It is how the category actually behaves, and the pattern is worth naming because it will happen to you again on a different vendor.

Ad tooling gets acquired, folded into a suite, and loses its own pricing page — while every article ranking for its name keeps describing the standalone product. The running list is long enough to be a genre:

  • Adtomic is now Helium 10 Ads. The old pricing URL is gone and the word appears nowhere on Helium 10's pricing page, yet plenty of ranking articles still describe a monthly add-on that no longer exists.
  • ChannelAdvisor is now Rithum, after being taken private in 2022 and rebranded in December 2023.
  • Downstream was absorbed into Jungle Scout's Cobalt.
  • Prestozon went into Helium 10; access to the standalone tool ended at the close of 2021.
  • Sunken Stone relaunched as Emplicit. Orca Pacific went into S4 Capital. Flywheel Digital has been an Omnicom practice area since January 2024.
  • And Kenshoo became Skai.

In every one of those cases the public review corpus, the pricing figures in circulation, and the feature descriptions all describe a product that no longer exists in that form. Almost nothing ranking for those names says so.

The reusable habit is two questions, asked before you evaluate anything: does this product still trade under this name, and when was the review I am reading written? Both take under a minute and both routinely change the answer.

What to do with a Kenshoo-era shortlist

If Kenshoo is on your list, the list was probably assembled some years ago or from an article that was. Rebuild it around what the products are now rather than what they were called then.

  • Re-scope the requirement first. Retail media has changed shape since 2021 — clean-room analysis, streaming inventory and off-Amazon exchange supply are all now ordinary parts of the conversation and were not then.
  • Re-check which vendors publish a price. Skai publishes annual tiers banded by media spend. Pacvue's pricing URL returns a not-found page. Quartile, Criteo, Rithum and Tinuiti quote on a call. That distribution has moved and it will move again.
  • Re-read the terms, not the feature grid. Renewal length, notice periods, fee-change mechanics and data portability decide more about the next two years than any capability comparison. Quartile's published terms describe a one-year auto-renewing arrangement with 60 days' notice and fees payable in advance — worth reading as an example of how much sits in a document nobody cites.
  • Ask about change of control. Skai was reported to have held M&A talks in 2024 that did not conclude. Nothing follows from that about the product; what follows for every vendor including us is that price protection and data portability belong in the contract before you need them.

The requirement most stale shortlists get wrong

Shortlists assembled a few years ago tend to be built around reporting consolidation: one console, one taxonomy, one export. That was the pressing problem when Kenshoo was the name on the box, and it is a real one.

The pressing problem now is granularity you can act on. Two figures from our own book make the point, both verified from a live Amazon DSP API pull across 27 advertisers over 31 days in summer 2026, both portfolio aggregates with no single advertiser identified.

18.3% of live DSP line items consumed 80% of the spend in that month. Concentration like that is not a fault; it is what an optimising system does. But it means a platform that shows you an account-level average is showing you the behaviour of a small minority of your line items, and any decision taken on that average is being taken about the wrong things.

And the trap that hides inside a good-looking dashboard: third-party mobile in-app video generated 48.3% of every click in our DSP book and 3.5% of the sales. A console optimising toward clicks, or a report where clicks are the headline, will point you confidently at the least productive inventory in the buy. The fix is not a better console. It is a person or a rule that looks at supply-source performance and acts on it.

So the question to put to any platform demo, or any agency including us: show me the supply-source report, and tell me who reads it and how often. That question would have been unusual in 2021. It is the centre of the evaluation now.

Who should license Skai, and who should not

License Skai if you have named in-house people whose actual job is to drive a platform, your media spans several retailers and channels, and consolidating them is the problem you are paying to solve. Their publisher breadth and their published price ladder are both genuine advantages, and no service relationship replaces a console your team wants to operate. On price transparency they are ahead of our own products, and we will say so.

Do not license anything if the honest answer to "who logs in daily" is a role rather than a person. A licence bought without an operator is a fixed cost driven at a fraction of its capacity, renewed because nobody wants to call the first year what it was.

Common questions

Is Kenshoo still a company?

The product is now called Skai. The rebrand happened in June 2021 and the legal entity is unchanged — the footer on skai.io still reads "© 2026 Kenshoo, Ltd." So the company continues to trade; only the name on the product changed.

Why are there no Kenshoo reviews on G2?

Because the record moved with the name. The listing titled Kenshoo Infinity Suite shows no rating and no reviews as of 21 August 2026, while the Skai listing carries 4.0 out of 5 across 297 reviews. An empty listing under a retired name is a filing artefact, not a signal about the product.

Can I trust old Kenshoo pricing figures?

No. Any figure attached to the retired name prices a product that has since been repositioned. Skai publishes current tiers on its own pricing page, banded by annual media spend. Read those directly and confirm the billing basis before you budget from anything.

What other product names in this category are out of date?

Adtomic is now Helium 10 Ads, ChannelAdvisor is Rithum, Downstream is inside Jungle Scout Cobalt, Prestozon went into Helium 10, Sunken Stone relaunched as Emplicit, Orca Pacific went into S4 Capital, and Flywheel Digital has been an Omnicom practice area since January 2024. Check the name before you evaluate the product.

What should I ask on a platform demo now that I would not have asked in 2021?

Show me the supply-source report, and tell me who reads it and how often. Consolidated reporting was the 2021 problem; acting on granularity is the current one.
