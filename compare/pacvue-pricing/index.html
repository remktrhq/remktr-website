Pacvue does not publish pricing. We checked: pacvue.com/pricing returns a 404 and every route on the site ends at Book a Demo. Expect a quote built from a platform minimum plus a percentage of monthly ad spend, but Pacvue has confirmed no figure publicly.

We looked. There is nothing to find

No pricing link in the navigation. No figure on any product page. The URL people keep linking to, pacvue.com/pricing, returns a 404 rather than a page. Every path through the site converges on the same button.

Third-party sites will happily fill the vacuum. Review aggregators, procurement marketplaces and agency blogs all carry estimates, usually some version of a monthly floor plus a low single-digit percentage of ad spend, rising for DSP and add-on modules. We are deliberately not reprinting those figures as if they were facts. None of them come from Pacvue, several contradict each other, and some are years old.

If you need a number for a budget line, get it from Pacvue in writing. If you need a number for a decision this week, the structure below is more useful than a guessed digit.

Publishing no price is itself information

It is easy to read a missing price as evasion. Usually it is not. Enterprise commerce software is sold on seat counts, retailer coverage, module mix and spend volume, and any single published number would be wrong for most buyers or would anchor every negotiation against the vendor. Plenty of respectable companies work this way.

What it does tell you is the shape of the sale. Quote-only pricing means:

  • Your price is a function of what they learn about you on the call, so the order of disclosure matters.
  • There is room to negotiate, because there is no list price to defend.
  • Renewal is a second negotiation, and a good first-year number is not a good three-year number.
  • Comparison shopping is deliberately expensive in hours, which favours the incumbent.

Treat all four as facts about the process rather than complaints about the vendor.

The variable rate matters more than the headline fee

Most buyers negotiate hard on the fixed monthly number and wave through the percentage attached to spend. That is backwards.

Take a brand spending $100,000 a month on Amazon ads. A single percentage point of that is $1,000 a month, or $12,000 a year. One point. Undisclosed, it never appears in the comparison spreadsheet next to the platform fee, and it compounds every time you scale spend — the fee grows precisely when you are trying to grow volume.

So the questions that matter are the boring ones. What exactly is the percentage? Is it applied to gross media or net? Does it tier down as spend rises, and at what thresholds? Does Amazon DSP spend carry the same rate as sponsored ads, or a higher one? Is there a floor that applies in a slow month?

Get those five answers in the contract and the headline fee stops being the interesting part of the deal.

Turning the demo into a pricing conversation

A demo is a product tour by default. You can convert it into a commercial one with a short agenda sent beforehand.

  • Ask for the fee structure in writing before the screen share, not after.
  • Name your real spend and your projected spend in twelve months, and ask for both prices.
  • Ask which modules are bundled and which are separate line items — commerce operations, digital shelf, DSP and market intelligence are commonly split.
  • Ask what the retailer coverage costs. Amazon only is one price; Amazon plus Walmart, Target and Instacart is another.
  • Ask what happens on renewal, and whether the rate is fixed for the term.

You will learn more from how those questions are answered than from the dashboard walkthrough.

Common questions

Why does pacvue.com/pricing return a 404?

Because Pacvue routes pricing through sales rather than a page. That is a deliberate enterprise motion, not a broken link to report. The practical consequence is that any figure you read elsewhere is somebody's recollection of a quote, not a list price.

What is a realistic Pacvue budget?

We will not print a number we cannot verify against Pacvue's own site. Ask them for a written fee structure covering the fixed component, the percentage of spend, the modules included, and the retailer coverage. Those four inputs determine your budget far more than any published estimate would.

Does Pacvue charge extra for Amazon DSP?

DSP is commonly described as an add-on rather than a base inclusion, but Pacvue does not confirm this publicly. Ask directly whether DSP spend carries the same percentage as sponsored ads, since a different rate on your largest growth channel changes the maths materially.

Is software or a managed service cheaper?

Neither, reliably. Compare like for like: licence plus creative plus the loaded cost of the person operating it, against a single managed percentage. The cheaper option is whichever one actually gets run, which is usually a staffing question rather than a pricing one.

Can I negotiate Pacvue pricing?

Quote-only pricing implies negotiation is expected. Volume, multi-year terms and module bundles are the usual levers. Ask what the rate looks like at your projected spend, not just your current spend, and get renewal terms fixed in the first contract.
