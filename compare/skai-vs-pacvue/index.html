Skai vs Pacvue: An Honest Split of Two Good Platforms

Skai is the cross-channel platform: retail media, paid search and paid social in one login, with annual tiers published on its own site. Pacvue is retail-media native and deeper on marketplace operations, priced by quote only. Pick Skai to compare channels, Pacvue to run many retailers.

The difference that actually decides it

Ignore feature grids for a moment. These two products were built to answer different questions.

Skai exists so a marketing team can hold Amazon, Google, Meta and a long tail of publishers in one view and ask which of them deserves next quarter's money. Its centre of gravity is planning and cross-channel measurement.

Pacvue exists so a commerce team can operate many retailers at once — bids, budgets, share of shelf, inventory signals, bulk rules — with Amazon as the anchor and Walmart, Target, Instacart and the rest alongside. Its centre of gravity is execution across marketplaces.

If the recurring conversation in your business is "how did retail media perform against social", Skai. If it is "why did our Walmart bids drift again", Pacvue. Most other differences follow from that.

Price transparency: the cleanest split between them

Skai publishes named tiers with dollar figures on its own pricing page — Standard, Advanced, Enterprise, Enterprise Premier, each capped by an annual media spend band, with a quoted tier above $35M. You can read the ladder before you speak to anyone.

Pacvue publishes nothing. Its pricing URL returns a 404 and every route on the site ends at a demo request. Third parties fill the gap with estimates that disagree with each other; we are not going to reprint them as fact.

Neither approach is dishonest. But they cost you different things. With Skai you can rule yourself in or out in five minutes. With Pacvue the cheapest path to a number is a call, which is a real cost when you are running a shortlist of six vendors.

Note the trap in either case: the fee attached to spend is where the money is. At $100,000 a month in ad spend, one undisclosed percentage point is $12,000 a year. Ask for the percentage, the base it applies to, and the thresholds where it steps down.

Where each one is genuinely stronger

Skai is stronger on: breadth of publisher and channel integration, historical comparison across channels, unified reporting for people who have to defend a whole media budget, and a pricing conversation you can start from a published number. Higher tiers add incrementality testing, which matters if measurement is the reason you are shopping at all.

Pacvue is stronger on: marketplace-native automation, retailer coverage, digital shelf and commerce operations sitting next to media, and the granular rule-writing that people who live in Amazon all day tend to want. Its Amazon workflows feel built for Amazon rather than adapted to it.

On reviews, read the sample sizes rather than the star ratings. One of these products has hundreds of public reviews accumulated over many years and multiple product generations; the other has far fewer. Averages built on very different sample sizes are not comparable, and a rating from a platform version three years ago tells you little about the one you would buy.

What both of them leave to you

Three costs sit outside either licence, and they are the same three in both cases:

  • Media. Every advertising dollar is on top of the software.
  • Creative. Display and video assets still need making, sizing and refreshing. Neither platform produces them.
  • The person. Both tools multiply the effectiveness of a competent operator. Neither supplies one. A licence with nobody accountable to it produces very well-formatted evidence of a problem nobody has time to fix.

That third line is the one that decides most of these projects, and it almost never appears in the comparison spreadsheet.

Common questions

Is Skai or Pacvue better for Amazon specifically?

Pacvue, in most cases. It is retail-media native and its Amazon workflows were built for marketplace use cases rather than adapted from a search-and-social heritage. Skai wins the moment Amazon has to be compared against other channels in the same view.

Which is cheaper?

Unanswerable honestly, because only one of them publishes a price. Skai's ladder is readable on its site; Pacvue quotes per account. Compare total cost of ownership instead — licence, creative, and the loaded cost of whoever runs it — rather than headline fees.

Can either one replace an agency?

Only if you have the people. Both are platforms, not operators. Teams that already employ traders get real leverage from software. Teams where advertising is one item on a busy person's list usually get better outcomes from a managed service at the same total cost.

Do both support Amazon DSP?

Both connect to Amazon DSP. Connection is not the same as capability — audience design, creative rotation and incrementality reading are what determine whether display pays. Ask each vendor to show a DSP account being managed, not a DSP tab being displayed.

What should I ask on both demos?

The percentage of ad spend, the base it applies to, whether DSP carries a different rate, the thresholds where it tiers down, and what renewal looks like. Then ask who on your side will use the platform daily, and be honest about the answer.
