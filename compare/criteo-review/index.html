Composable or monolithic: what the published documents show

A monolithic platform is one product, one contract and one data model that you take whole. A composable one is built from parts you can buy, swap or leave out. Real vendors sit on a spectrum, and the honest way to place one is to read what it publishes rather than what a review site infers.

Criteo's own materials point both ways. On the unified side, it sells Commerce Max as a single platform under one set of terms. On the modular side, those same Commerce Max terms of service name a Demand Side Platform Fee and, for managed campaigns, a Managed Service Fee, each as a percentage of working media spend, plus retailer audience data charges and possible third-party technology fees. Its site also says its demand is reachable from 60+ third-party demand-side platforms, which is an open connection rather than a closed one.

Our reading is that it is a unified platform with modular commercial lines. That is a reading of public documents, not an insider view of the architecture, so treat it as a starting point for questions.

  • Can I buy the media and skip the managed service? The terms list them as separate fees, so ask how they are quoted.
  • Can I use my own DSP or a partner's against the same retailer inventory? The site describes third-party DSP access; ask which retailers it covers.
  • Can I export my data if I leave? Composable only helps if the pieces come with you.

Is Criteo a unicorn? Why that label does not fit

The other search this page appears for is whether Criteo is a unicorn. The term normally means a privately held start-up valued at $1 billion or more. Criteo is a Nasdaq-listed company that has reported to public investors for years, so by the usual definition it is not one, whatever its valuation.

The more useful fact is how the market has priced it recently. Bloomberg reported on 6 July 2026, and Reuters reported the same day citing two people familiar with the matter, that Vista Equity Partners and Quinti Capital had approached Criteo at a premium of more than 50% to recent trading. According to that coverage, Nasdaq-listed shares closed 21.4% higher at $23.17, a market value of roughly $1.16 billion. None of the parties confirmed terms, Criteo's board had not said how it would respond, and any completed transaction would need shareholder approval. It is reported, not settled.

Secondary coverage has also circulated a much larger equity figure that does not reconcile with the share count. That is the failure mode we describe below: a confident number repeated onward without anyone re-deriving it.

None of this is a reason to avoid a vendor. Ad tech consolidates constantly, and every firm on your shortlist carries a version of the same exposure. It is a reason to negotiate three clauses in any multi-year agreement: termination without penalty on a change of control, a price-protection period that survives an ownership change, and a data-portability commitment. Ask all three of every vendor, including us.

What buyer reviews measure, and why ratings are hard to read

Star ratings for advertising platforms are written by the people who log in. They are useful for narrow questions: is the interface navigable, does reporting export, does support answer, how long did onboarding take. They cannot carry the question a finance director asks: did the spend produce sales that would not have happened anyway. That is a property of an experiment, and it depends on your category, margins, creative and competitors.

There is also a selection effect that applies to every vendor, including us: the people invited to review are usually the ones the vendor is comfortable inviting. That is normal commercial behaviour, but it means the text carries more information than the average.

We learned the second problem from our own mistake. We once published a page on another vendor that recommended the product on the strength of an aggregate rating pulled from a site that collects scores from several platforms. When we opened the platforms it claimed to aggregate, the numbers could not be reconciled with it. The rating was the argument of the page, so we deleted the page rather than correcting it. Three habits came out of that:

  • A score without its count is not a fact. Take both together, and treat a small count as a set of anecdotes.
  • Open the platform that issues the number, not the site that repeats it.
  • Date the review, then verify support on a reference call. Many reviews of large ad-tech platforms predate the current product and sometimes the current owner.

The primary sources that beat any rating

Criteo is unusually documentable, which is an advantage worth taking.

Its own site states the scale: 200+ retailers and premium media owners, 17,000+ global clients, 740M daily active users, 5B product SKUs, access from 60+ third-party demand-side platforms and 100k algorithm tests a year. Those are company claims rather than audited figures, but they are published and attributable, a different class of evidence from an anonymous testimonial.

It also writes its commercial architecture into the Commerce Max terms described above. No rates appear, because those live in your order form, but knowing the shape of the invoice before a call is worth more than a star rating, and few competitors document it at all.

Third, it is publicly listed and reports on a schedule, so two quarters of results tell you more about the health of a network you are about to depend on than any review site. And its advertiser pages now describe incrementality testing, so anyone telling you the platform cannot measure lift is describing an older product.

The principle matters more than the vendor: read what a company is obliged to say before you read what its customers volunteered.

What the 2026 record shows about direction

A review dated last year describes a product that has since moved, so read the announcements rather than the retrospectives.

On the network side, Criteo published an expanded partnership with Loblaw Advance in August 2026, an onsite retail media partnership with Lidl in May 2026, and a South African expansion with Massmart in July 2026. On the product side, it published two updates on its integration with OpenAI, in May and June 2026. In June 2026 it was named a leader in an analyst matrix for retail media network and monetisation platforms, and in July 2026 it completed a redomiciliation from France to Luxembourg.

That is a company investing in network breadth and new surfaces. It also points to a neutral piece of diligence: retail media networks are commercial relationships that get renewed, expanded or ended. Before you sign, confirm that the retailers you care about, in the countries you sell in, are live today, and ask what notice you would receive if one changed.

Five checks worth more than the ratings

Run these before you commit budget. They work for any commerce media platform, composable or not.

  • Named retailer coverage. List your top five retail relationships by revenue and ask for written confirmation that each is live, in your markets, with the ad formats you want.
  • Fee denominator. Ask what each percentage is charged on, and whether the budget you state is inclusive of fees. The same rate on two denominators is two prices.
  • Unbundling. Ask which fee lines can be removed, and what the platform does without them.
  • Prospecting against retargeting, separately. Ask what share of a comparable account's spend reached shoppers who have never seen the brand, and what each segment returned on its own. A blended figure hides the answer.
  • Proof design. Ask how they would demonstrate incremental sales rather than attributed sales, who would build the holdout, and whether the design goes into the scope of work before spend starts.

Also read the termination clause before the pricing. A vendor who answers most of these crisply has taught you more in an hour than a week of reviews.

Who Criteo suits, and where reMKTR fits

Criteo suits brands selling through many retailers in many countries who want shopper audiences bought across those networks under one contract. That is a real capability, and an Amazon specialist is not a substitute for it. Retailers and marketplaces monetising their own inventory are a different customer buying a different product from the same company, and we do not compete there.

It fits less well in the narrow case we are built for: a brand whose revenue is overwhelmingly one marketplace, whose real question is whether display added incremental orders there. That gets answered against that marketplace's own purchase data, in a clean room, with something held out, by someone not paid on the answer.

reMKTR buys Amazon DSP, the demand-side platform and not the Delivery Service Partner courier programme, as a managed service on our own seats. We are part of Full Circle, a full-service Amazon management company with 100+ brands managed and $500M+ in Amazon revenue managed. Across our measured portfolio of 30 advertisers in July 2026, display delivered 6.04x on ad spend at a $5.49 cost per acquisition across 57,137 attributed purchases, on 78.4 million impressions, with 20.1% of purchases coming from shoppers new to the brand.

We reconcile in Amazon Marketing Cloud, where display and sponsored ads stop double-counting each other. On one account, a publishing brand, organic order share went from 13.5% before display started to 27.9% afterwards, while sponsored-ads cost of sale compressed from 48% to 37%. One account, one window, told as a story rather than a benchmark.

Ask any platform or agency how quiet weeks get reported, and whether the bad ones are reported first. If the problem is broader than media, such as listings or availability, Full Circle is the right door.

Which one you should actually pick

Criteo suits multi-retailer brands wanting one contract across many networks, and it documents its fee lines and roadmap more openly than most of this category. Review sites will tell you about the interface and little else. reMKTR suits Amazon-led brands who want display bought for them and proof, against a holdout, that it added orders.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you need done, not the feature list. Ask how they would prove display added sales: a holdout set before launch, and DSP and sponsored ads reconciled in Amazon Marketing Cloud. A partner who can only show last-click ROAS is showing you sales your search ads may have won anyway.

Common questions

Is Criteo composable or monolithic?

Neither in the pure sense. Its published terms sell one platform but itemise separate fees for the platform, managed service and data, and its site describes access from third-party DSPs. Ask which pieces you can buy without the others.

Is Criteo a unicorn?

Not in the usual sense of the word. A unicorn is a privately held start-up valued at $1 billion or more, and Criteo is a Nasdaq-listed company. Reuters coverage in July 2026 put its market value at roughly $1.16 billion after reported takeover interest.

Is Criteo any good?

For its intended job, buying shopper audiences across a large network of retailers and the open internet, it is a serious, established platform with published scale. Whether it is good for you depends on how much of your revenue sits outside the retailer where your demand concentrates.

Does Criteo work for Amazon sellers?

It can drive traffic to a brand's own site and to other retailers, but Amazon's own placements are bought through Amazon DSP. If Amazon is where the sales happen, the platform holding the purchase signal and the clean-room measurement is the one that can show what display did.

Should takeover reporting change my decision?

Not by itself. Reporting is not a completed transaction, and consolidation is a feature of this market. Convert the worry into contract terms: termination without penalty on a change of control, a price-protection period, and data portability. Ask every vendor on your list.
