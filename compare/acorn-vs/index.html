Which comparison you are actually running

Before anything else: this is Acorn-i, the Amazon and ecommerce specialist, not Acorns the investing app and not Acorn at New Engen, which sells creator marketing. Confusing the last two produces a shortlist where one candidate cannot do the job at all.

With that settled, three matchups account for nearly every version of this question.

  • Specialist against holding company. Acorn-i inside The Brandtech Group against Flywheel inside Omnicom. This is really a question about scale and account seniority.
  • Full-service against single-channel depth. Either of them against a partner that only buys one thing and is measured on it. That is us.
  • Media against content. Acorn-i against Acorn at New Engen, which is not a comparison at all — one buys media, the other supplies creators.

The rest of this page takes the first two seriously, states what each side is genuinely good at, and says plainly where each one beats the others.

Acorn-i: technology-led, European-centred, inside a group

Founded in 2018 by Amazon alumni, majority-acquired by The Brandtech Group in 2022 and later fused with Jellyfish to form Jellyfish Commerce. Its own site describes an agency built around Ignite, an analytics application that consolidates marketplace, Amazon, DTC and advertising data into one view.

The strength is real and specific: an operating culture that starts from account data rather than from a media plan. Amazon Ads published a case study crediting Acorn-i's hourly retail analytics on the pet food brand Naturediet with a 27% increase in ROAS in Q1 2023 and a 90% cut in campaign optimisation time. Whatever you make of any single case study, the method behind it — reading retail performance by hour and acting inside the day — is a genuinely more demanding way to run an account than weekly bid reviews.

Who they suit better than us: brands whose Amazon business spans the UK and several European marketplaces, who want the digital shelf, the content and the media handled by one team with a group behind it. We are not that, and pretending otherwise would waste your time.

Flywheel: the commerce practice with the widest reach

Omnicom acquired Flywheel from Ascential for a net cash price of approximately $835 million, announced at the end of October 2023 and closed at the start of January 2024. Omnicom's own release described a business of more than 2,000 professionals serving over 4,500 brands across retail commerce operations, media execution and market intelligence, led by Duncan Painter.

What that buys a client is coverage and continuity: hundreds of marketplaces, a market intelligence heritage that predates the retail media boom, procurement and governance processes built for enterprise, and the ability to staff a global account without subcontracting. For a brand running Amazon, Walmart, Target and international marketplaces simultaneously, that breadth is not a nice-to-have; it is the product.

Who they suit better than us: enterprise advertisers who need one contract covering many retailers and many countries, with the compliance and reporting apparatus a large organisation requires. If your review process involves procurement, information security and six stakeholders, a firm of that scale is built for it and we are not.

The honest trade — and it applies to every large practice, including the large parts of our own group — is that seniority gets allocated. Ask which named people will be on your account weekly, not which people presented in the pitch.

reMKTR: narrower, and measured on one thing

We buy Amazon DSP — the demand-side platform for programmatic display, video and audio, not Delivery Service Partner, the courier franchise — as a managed service on our own seats. We are part of Full Circle, a full-service Amazon management company with $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands, and we hold 109 live Amazon DSP advertiser seats.

The July 2026 read across 30 of those advertisers: 6.04x return on ad spend for the whole set rather than the best line item, 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM and a blended $1.42 cost per click, $5.49 cost per acquisition across 57,137 attributed purchases, 20.1% of them new to the brand.

The mechanism behind the number matters more than the number. Last-click attribution cannot prove incrementality and never could; holdouts and matched controls can. We reconcile in Amazon Marketing Cloud, where DSP and sponsored ads stop double-counting each other — which is how you find out that some share of display-attributed revenue was already being claimed by search, and how you decide what display is really worth.

What we do not do: catalogue and content operations across many European marketplaces, creator sourcing, or a single contract covering four retailers on three continents. On those, the other two are better answers.

Two honest redirects. Dr. DSP when you want that same DSP capability as a product your team runs rather than a service you hire. Full Circle when the constraint is wider than media — listings, catalogue, supply chain — and display is only one symptom.

The three axes, and the question that tests each one

Every candidate in this category can be placed on three axes. Score them and the shortlist usually resolves itself.

  • Scale. How many retailers, markets and languages can they cover under one contract without subcontracting? Test question: name the last account you ran in each of my three markets, and who staffed it.
  • Seniority. How much of the senior thinking survives the pitch? Test question: which named individuals touch my account weekly, and what is their span of other accounts?
  • Proof. Can they show that advertising added sales rather than recorded them? Test question: describe the design of the last incrementality test you ran — what was held out, for how long, and what did you conclude when it did not work?

The third question is the one that separates candidates fastest, and it is fair to put to us as well. A partner who answers it with a platform-attributed ROAS chart is answering a different question.

Running the bake-off without rigging it

Comparisons in this category usually fail on process rather than judgement. Four rules keep it fair.

Give every candidate the same brief and the same data access. A firm working from a fuller export will look smarter for reasons that have nothing to do with capability.

Agree the measurement definition before anyone starts. Attributed sales, incremental sales and total account sales tell three different stories about the same fortnight, and whichever one you accept in the pitch is the one you will be arguing about in month nine.

Set the same window — ninety days is usually the shortest honest read for display — and the same definition of failure. Ask each candidate to write down in advance what result would make them recommend stopping.

Finally, price last. A quote is meaningless until you know what is inside it: whether media is inclusive of fees, what creative production costs, and whether the seat is yours or theirs.

Which one you should actually pick

Acorn-i suits multi-market European Amazon businesses wanting analytics-led execution with a group behind it. Flywheel suits enterprise brands needing one contract across many retailers and countries. reMKTR suits Amazon-led brands with a display budget nobody has yet proved incremental. Score all three on scale, seniority and proof before price.

What to do with this

Neither of these decides your ACoS on its own — how much of the work gets done each week does. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above it was 33.6%. Pick the option that leaves someone actually working that list, whether that is you or us.

Common questions

Is Acorn-i still a separate company from Jellyfish?

A majority stake went to The Brandtech Group in 2022, and the business was subsequently merged into Jellyfish under the Jellyfish Commerce banner, with acorn-i.com still live. If you are contracting, ask which entity signs, which team is assigned and whether the people in the pitch are the people on the account.

Is a large agency network worse than a specialist?

No — it is different. A network gives you coverage, redundancy and process that a small firm cannot fund, and that genuinely matters for complex global accounts. A specialist gives you senior attention on a narrow problem. The failure mode of the first is dilution; of the second, gaps. Pick according to which risk you can least afford.

Can I compare an agency against a software platform in the same process?

Only if you add the operator's salary to the software side and count the media on both. Otherwise you are comparing a licence to a team. The cleanest way is total cost of ownership over twelve months, set against what each side can prove about incremental sales.

Which of the three is best for a first Amazon DSP campaign?

Whoever will run the first test properly. A first display campaign should be designed as an experiment: a clear audience thesis, a holdout, a stated read date and an agreed definition of failure. Any of the three can do that; ask each one to describe it in advance and see who does.

How much should I weight case studies?

Less than the method behind them. Case studies are selected by the agency and describe accounts that are not yours. What travels between accounts is process — how often bids and placements are reviewed, how creative rotation is decided, how a test is designed. Ask about the process that produced the case study rather than the result.
