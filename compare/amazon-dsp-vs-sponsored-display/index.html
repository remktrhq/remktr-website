Amazon DSP vs Sponsored Display: Which Retargeting Actually Scales?

Updated 2026-08-29 · 2392 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon dsp vs sponsored display”
The short answer

Sponsored Display is a self-service ad product: no minimum spend, packaged audiences, creative mostly generated from your listing. Amazon DSP is the full programmatic platform: custom audiences, streaming TV and video inventory, real frequency control, clean-room measurement — behind a seat with real entry requirements. Sponsored Display is where retargeting starts. DSP is what it grows into when the packaged version runs out of levers.

Two products that share signals, not much else

The confusion is understandable: both put display ads in front of Amazon shoppers, both retarget product-page visitors, both reach audiences on and off Amazon, and both draw on the same underlying shopping signals. From there they diverge.

Sponsored Display lives in the advertising console next to Sponsored Products. It is available to sellers enrolled in Brand Registry and to vendors, has no minimum spend, and bills either cost-per-click or per thousand viewable impressions depending on the optimisation you choose. You pick from packaged audiences — views and purchases remarketing, in-market, lifestyle and interest segments — or contextual product targeting, and Amazon assembles most of the creative from your listing.

Amazon DSP is a demand-side platform — the same class of tool trading desks use — for programmatic display, online video, streaming TV and audio, on Amazon and across the wider web and apps. It is reached through a seat: Amazon's managed service, described by Amazon as typically requiring a USD 50,000 minimum investment that varies by country; your own self-service access, agreed with Amazon Ads; or an agency's seat, on the agency's terms. And a DSP campaign can run whether or not you sell on Amazon at all — Sponsored Display cannot.

One disambiguation, because search mixes them: Amazon DSP here is the demand-side platform, not the Delivery Service Partner courier programme. Same initials, different world.

Audiences: picking from a menu versus writing the recipe

Sponsored Display's audiences are genuinely useful and genuinely fixed. Views remarketing rebuilds an audience of shoppers who saw your product pages — or similar products — within a preset lookback you select. Purchases remarketing does the same for past buyers. In-market, lifestyle and interest segments come as Amazon defines them. What you cannot do is edit the recipe: no custom ASIN pools mixing your products your way, no exclusion logic beyond the basics, no layering one signal on another.

DSP is the recipe book. A retargeting pool of viewers of these six ASINs, minus purchasers of anything in the brand in the last 90 days, minus subscribers, capped at this frequency, suppressed in these weeks — that sentence is a normal Tuesday on DSP and unwritable in Sponsored Display. The difference matters most in exclusions: at small budgets, showing an ad to someone who already bought is a rounding error; at scale, unexcluded audiences quietly spend a meaningful share of the budget buying back customers you already own.

Placements: the gap is video-shaped

Sponsored Display serves display placements on product pages and across Amazon, and extends to placements off Amazon — with Amazon choosing where within its network your ads appear. For product-page defence and simple off-Amazon reach, that is a fair trade of control for convenience.

DSP's inventory is a different category: streaming TV through Amazon's properties and partnerships, online video, audio, Twitch, Fire TV, and display across the open web and apps — with you, not the algorithm, deciding which environments run and which sit on the exclusion list. If the media plan says "video" anywhere on it, the comparison is over: Sponsored Display is not where streaming campaigns live. Our note on what that inventory actually includes is in which streaming services Amazon DSP can reach.

Creative control: generated versus produced

Sponsored Display's default creative is assembled from your listing — image, title, price, badge — with options to add a headline, logo, custom image or video. It is fast, compliant by construction, and looks like what it is. DSP expects produced assets across display sizes and video lengths, in exchange for full control of what the shopper actually sees: lifestyle creative, seasonal versions, tested variants. That control is only worth paying for if you can feed it — a DSP seat with one static banner is a sports car in first gear, which is why creative capacity belongs in the decision, not after it.

Measurement: where the products stop being comparable

Sponsored Display reports clicks, viewable impressions, attributed sales and new-to-brand metrics in the console. For a self-service product, that reporting is respectable — you can see what the packaged audiences did.

DSP reports in its own console too, but its real measurement story is Amazon Marketing Cloud: path analysis, audience overlap, designed holdouts, and — the one that bites hardest — deduplication. Because each Amazon ad product attributes its own sales independently, a shopper who saw your DSP ad and clicked your Sponsored Display ad can be counted by both. Running both products is often correct; summing their dashboards never is. The mechanics are laid out in why DSP and sponsored ads double-count each other.

When Sponsored Display is genuinely enough

An honest list, because the cheapest correct answer is a fine answer.

  • Retargeting budgets under a few thousand a month. The packaged pools do the job, and no seat fee or minimum stands between you and starting today.
  • Product-page defence. Holding your own detail pages and conquesting adjacent ones is Sponsored Display's home turf.
  • No creative pipeline. If produced video and multi-size display are not realistic this quarter, DSP's main advantages sit unused.
  • Testing whether display moves anything at all. A cheap Sponsored Display test is a legitimate first experiment before anyone signs anything.

When DSP unlocks what Sponsored Display cannot

  • Streaming TV and online video. There is no Sponsored Display route to these. If upper-funnel video is in the plan, DSP is the door.
  • Custom audience logic. Exclusions, suppression windows, layered pools, first-party signals — the levers that keep scaled retargeting from decaying into buying your own customers back.
  • Frequency control. At real budgets, unmanaged frequency is a silent tax; DSP caps it where you say.
  • Off-Amazon and non-Amazon destinations. DSP can run for brands whose revenue is mostly DTC, and can send traffic off Amazon — a different game entirely.
  • Clean-room measurement. If leadership is asking whether the media is incremental, only the DSP-plus-AMC stack can answer with a designed test instead of a dashboard.

    Which one you should actually pick

    Pick Sponsored Display for modest-budget retargeting, page defence, and any test you want running this afternoon. Pick Amazon DSP when the plan includes video, custom audience logic, frequency control, off-Amazon destinations or clean-room measurement — the things the packaged product structurally cannot do. Running both is normal; reporting them as a naive sum is not.

    What to do with this

    Write the media plan first and let the products fall out of it. If every line is retargeting under a few thousand a month, open the console and start — no seat required. The moment a line says "streaming", "exclude existing customers" or "prove incrementality", you are shopping for a DSP route, and the comparison worth your time is between routes and partners, not between the two ad products.

    Common questions

    Is Sponsored Display the same as Amazon DSP?

    No. Sponsored Display is a self-service ad product inside the advertising console, bought like Sponsored Products with no minimum spend. Amazon DSP is a full demand-side platform for programmatic display, video and audio, reached through a seat — Amazon's managed service, your own self-service access, or an agency's. They share underlying audience signals but differ in control, placements, creative and measurement.

    Do I need Amazon DSP if I already run Sponsored Display?

    Not necessarily. If your goals are product-page retargeting and category defence at modest budgets, Sponsored Display may genuinely be enough. DSP earns its complexity when you need custom audience construction, streaming TV and online video inventory, frequency management across channels, or clean-room measurement in Amazon Marketing Cloud.

    Which is better for retargeting?

    Both retarget. Sponsored Display offers packaged views and purchases remarketing with lookback windows you select from preset options. DSP lets you define the pools: which ASINs, which windows, which exclusions, layered with purchase suppression and frequency caps you control. At small scale the packaged version performs respectably; as budgets grow, control over who is excluded starts to matter more than who is included.

    What does each cost to start?

    Sponsored Display has no minimum — it bills cost-per-click or per thousand viewable impressions, and you can start with a few dollars a day. Amazon DSP's managed service is described by Amazon as typically requiring a USD 50,000 minimum investment, varying by country; agency seats set their own lower floors; self-service terms are agreed with Amazon Ads. Fee structures for managed routes vary by partner.

    Will DSP and Sponsored Display double-count each other's sales?

    They can. Each product reports its own attributed sales independently, so one purchase can be claimed by both. Running both is often the right strategy, but reconcile the combined figure in Amazon Marketing Cloud rather than summing two dashboards.
