What Amazon streaming TV ads are

Streaming TV ads are the video ads that play in the breaks of streaming shows, movies and live events, on a television screen rather than a phone or laptop. Amazon sells them across inventory it owns, including Prime Video and Fire TV, and across streaming publishers that sell through Amazon's programmatic platform.

Two things make them different from the ads most Amazon sellers know. First, they are bought by CPM: you pay per thousand times the ad is shown, whether or not anyone clicks. Second, they sit at the top of the funnel. The viewer is on a couch watching a show, not typing a product search, so the job of the ad is awareness and consideration, not an immediate click.

That is why they behave nothing like Sponsored Products. A sponsored ad answers a shopper who is already searching. A streaming TV ad introduces your brand to people who have not searched yet.

Where they run and how you buy them

Amazon's streaming TV ads are sold through Amazon DSP, its demand-side platform. DSP is the programmatic buying tool for display, video and streaming TV, and it reaches audiences on Amazon-owned surfaces and on third-party sites and apps. The Amazon-owned streaming surfaces include Prime Video, Fire TV and Twitch. Check Amazon's own advertising pages for the current list, because the supply and the formats change over time.

In practice you have three routes:

  • Self-serve in DSP, if your team already has access and the time to build line items, audiences and frequency caps.
  • Managed through Amazon's own team, which Amazon has historically tied to a minimum investment. Confirm the current minimum with Amazon, because it is the first thing that decides which route is open to you.
  • Managed through a DSP service such as reMKTR, which runs Amazon DSP as a service on its own advertiser accounts for brands that do not want to build the in-house capability.

Targeting is one of the main reasons brands choose Amazon for streaming. Because Amazon can tie viewing and shopping signals to accounts, you can aim video at shopper audiences, such as people who browsed your category or your product pages, rather than buying a broad slice of a TV show and hoping.

What they cost: how to think about CPM

Amazon does not publish a fixed rate card for streaming TV, and prices are set in an auction or negotiated for direct deals. Any single CPM you read online is a snapshot, not a standing price, and live sports and premium content cost more than general on-demand inventory. Treat every number you see in a blog post, including ours, as a prompt to ask for current pricing.

What does not change is the arithmetic. Here is a worked example. The figures are an illustration, not a quote and not a reMKTR result:

  • A $30 CPM means $30 per 1,000 impressions, or $0.03 per impression.
  • Say a sale leaves you $15 after product cost and fees.
  • $15 divided by $0.03 is 500. You need one sale for roughly every 500 impressions just to break even on the media.

Run that calculation with your own CPM and your own margin before you launch. It tells you quickly whether streaming can plausibly pay back on direct sales alone, or whether you are really buying awareness that you will judge on other signals.

How to judge streaming TV: do not use last-click

This is the part the ad-format guides tend to skip. Streaming TV starts the path to purchase, and a different ad usually finishes it. A shopper sees your video on Prime Video, searches your brand name a day later, clicks a Sponsored Products ad and buys. Last-click gives all of the credit to the sponsored ad and none to the video.

So a dashboard return on ad spend for streaming will usually look weaker than for search, and that comparison is misleading. Judge streaming on the signals it is built to move:

  • Completion rate: how many viewers watched the full ad.
  • New-to-brand share: how many buyers were new to your brand. Across 30 advertisers in July 2026, 20.1% of our DSP portfolio's purchases came from shoppers new to the brand.
  • Branded search movement: more people typing your brand name in the weeks after a flight.
  • Incrementality: a holdout test, or DSP reconciled against sponsored ads in Amazon Marketing Cloud, so one purchase is not counted twice.

For context on our own numbers: across 30 advertisers in July 2026, the reMKTR DSP portfolio delivered 6.04x return on ad spend, 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM and a blended $5.49 cost per acquisition. That is the whole book across every placement type, not streaming alone, and not a promise about what your account will do. Set streaming its own target before you launch rather than holding it to the blended number.

A checklist before you launch

Use this list before you commit any budget to streaming TV on Amazon:

  • Write down the goal. Awareness, new customers or retargeting. Each one is judged differently.
  • Run the break-even calculation above with your own margin.
  • Check your creative. You need a finished video ad that meets Amazon's current specifications for streaming TV. Read the spec page for length, format and file requirements before production, not after.
  • Set a frequency cap so the same household is not shown the ad too many times in a week.
  • Plan a flight, not a single burst. Awareness compounds with repetition, so a one-off test will rarely tell you what a sustained flight would.
  • Agree how it will be measured before it runs, including who designs the test and who computes the result. That question applies to any vendor, including us.
  • Keep your search coverage healthy. A streaming campaign sends people to search. If your listings and branded Sponsored Products campaigns are not ready, you pay for the awareness and lose the sale.

Who streaming TV ads suit, and who should wait

They suit brands with a product that benefits from being seen and explained, such as a visual or considered purchase, and brands that already have search and listings in good shape and now need to reach new people. They also suit brands with a budget large enough to run a sustained flight and a team, or a partner, able to read the results beyond last-click.

They are probably not the next step for a seller still tuning Sponsored Products, or one whose listing conversion is weak. Fixing the search term report and the listing will usually return more per dollar than adding a top-of-funnel channel. If that is where you are, start with sponsored ads and come back to streaming when reach, not search, is the constraint.

Which one you should actually pick

Amazon streaming TV ads suit brands ready to buy reach beyond search, with a sustained budget, finished video creative and a plan to measure beyond last-click. Sellers still tuning Sponsored Products or listing conversion should start there. When streaming is the right next step, run it as a flight with its own target.

What to do with this

Whoever runs your DSP, a partner or your own team, ask them two things: how much of DSP's attributed sales also had a sponsored ads click, and how they would hold out part of the audience in month one. Back whoever answers with a method rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

What are Amazon streaming TV ads?

They are video ads that play inside streaming TV content on Amazon's owned surfaces, such as Prime Video and Fire TV, and on partner streaming publishers. They are bought by CPM through Amazon DSP.

How much do Amazon streaming TV ads cost?

Amazon does not publish a fixed rate card. Pricing comes from an auction or a negotiated deal, and premium or live content costs more than general inventory. Work out your break-even point per impression first, then ask for current pricing.

Can small sellers run streaming TV ads on Amazon?

It depends on the route. Self-serve DSP access and managed services each have their own requirements, and Amazon has tied its own managed service to a minimum investment. Check the current terms. Many small sellers get a better return from sponsored ads first.

Are Amazon streaming TV ads the same as Prime Video ads?

Prime Video ads are one part of it. Amazon's streaming TV ads also cover other Amazon-owned surfaces, such as Fire TV, and partner streaming publishers reached through DSP.

How do I know if streaming TV ads are working?

Do not rely on last-click return on ad spend. Look at completion rate, new-to-brand share, branded search movement and a holdout or Amazon Marketing Cloud analysis that shows what the ads added.
