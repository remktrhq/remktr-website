CTV Advertising: What It Is, How It Works and How to Measure It

The short answer

CTV advertising is paid video that plays inside streaming content on internet-connected TVs, such as smart TVs, streaming sticks and game consoles. Brands buy it by the thousand impressions (CPM), usually through a programmatic platform, and target households by audience rather than by TV channel.

What CTV advertising actually is

CTV stands for connected TV: any television that gets its video over the internet instead of over a cable or antenna feed. CTV advertising is the ad slot inside that video. It is usually a 15 or 30 second spot that plays before or during a show, a film or a live event on a streaming app, and it cannot be skipped in most cases.

Two terms get mixed up. CTV refers to the device: the television. OTT (over the top) refers to the delivery method: video sent over the internet, which also reaches phones and laptops. All CTV ads are OTT ads. Not every OTT ad is a CTV ad. When a vendor quotes you a price, ask which one they mean, because a phone-screen impression and a living-room impression are not the same product.

What makes CTV different from linear TV is the buying. A cable ad is bought against a channel and a time slot. A CTV ad is bought against an audience, and the platform decides which household sees it, on which app, at what price.

How CTV ads are bought and billed

There are three common routes to the same screen:

  • Direct from the publisher. You agree a deal with a streaming service for a set amount of inventory. This suits large, planned campaigns, including live sports.
  • Programmatic through a demand-side platform (DSP). You set a budget, an audience and a bid, and the platform buys impressions across many apps in real time. This is the route most brands start with.
  • Through the streaming service's own ad tools. Several large services sell self-serve ads on their own inventory.

Almost all CTV is billed on CPM, the price per thousand impressions. You pay when the ad is served, not when someone clicks, because a television has nothing to click. That is the main reason CTV is judged differently from search ads.

Quick arithmetic you can reuse: budget divided by CPM, times 1,000, equals impressions. At a $4.00 CPM, $10,000 buys 2,500,000 impressions. Premium streaming and live content costs more than that, so always check the CPM you are quoted against the placement you are actually getting.

Why brands use CTV: reach, targeting and the full-funnel role

CTV earns its place for three reasons.

  • Reach beyond people who are already searching. Search ads capture demand that exists. CTV creates awareness among people who have not yet searched for you.
  • Household-level targeting. Audiences can be built from shopping behaviour, interests or your own customer lists, instead of from a channel's general viewers.
  • A big-screen, sound-on environment. The creative gets full attention in a way a thumbnail on a feed does not.

The trade-off is that CTV sits at the top of the funnel. It is better at starting the purchase journey than finishing it. If you judge it on same-day sales from the ad itself, it will usually look weaker than a search campaign, which is bidding on the very last step before a purchase.

How to measure CTV without fooling yourself

This is the part most explainers skip. Because nobody clicks a TV ad, the usual click-based reports under-count what CTV does. Use these measures instead, roughly in this order:

  • Completion rate. The share of ad views watched to the end. It tells you whether the creative and the placement are holding attention.
  • Reach and frequency. How many households saw the ad and how many times. Too few views does nothing, and too many wastes budget.
  • Branded search and direct traffic. Does the volume of people searching your brand name rise in the days after a flight starts?
  • New-to-brand rate. What share of buyers had not purchased from you in the past year.
  • Incrementality. A holdout test: run the same audience with and without the ad and compare outcomes. It is the only measure that separates sales the ad caused from sales that would have happened anyway.

Ask who designs and computes any test, and whether it is written into the contract. That question applies to every vendor, us included.

A checklist before you buy CTV

Run through these questions with any platform or agency, including us:

  • What exactly am I buying: which apps, which devices, live or on-demand?
  • Is the CPM quoted for the placement I will actually get?
  • What audience will see the ad, and how is it built?
  • Are the ads delivered to the television screen, or to every device?
  • What frequency cap will be set?
  • What is the success measure for this line, and is it agreed before launch?
  • Will there be a holdout or lift test, and who runs it?
  • What fees sit on top of media, and will I get written notice if they change?

Starting small is reasonable. Run one test flight with a clear goal, read the result against that goal, and then decide whether to scale.

Who CTV suits, and when to wait

CTV suits brands that already have search and shopping campaigns working, want to reach new customers, and can wait longer than a few days to judge the effect. It also suits brands with a strong video or lifestyle story to tell.

It is usually too early for a brand still fixing its listings, its search term reports or its profitability per order. Fix those first. Common questions

What is CTV advertising in simple terms?

It is video advertising shown on a television that is connected to the internet, inside streaming apps. You choose who should see the ad, and you pay per thousand impressions.

What is the difference between CTV and OTT advertising?

CTV describes the device, which is a connected television. OTT describes the delivery, which is video sent over the internet to any screen. Every CTV ad is an OTT ad, but an OTT ad can also play on a phone or laptop.

How is CTV advertising priced?

Mostly by CPM, the cost per 1,000 impressions. Prices vary by audience, app, device and whether the content is live. Check the CPM you are quoted against the placement you will actually receive.

How do you measure CTV ad performance?

Start with completion rate, reach and frequency, then look at branded search, new-to-brand rate and sales lift. The strongest evidence is an incrementality test, which compares an audience that saw the ad with a holdout group that did not.
