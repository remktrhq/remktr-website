Repurposing One Video Across Streaming, OLV and Social

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1313 words · Written against what currently ranked for “Repurposing one video across streaming, OLV and social”
The short answer

A single shoot can supply streaming, OLV and social — but the export can't be identical across all three. Aspect ratio, audio-default assumptions, safe area and file specs all differ enough between non-skippable CTV and muted-first social feeds that a true one-size-fits-all export underperforms on at least one of them. Plan the shoot to capture what each cut will need, then build the versions deliberately.

What has to change, concretely

Aspect ratio is the most obvious difference — CTV requires 16:9 at a minimum 1920x1080 frame, while most social platforms favor vertical or square crops that a straight 16:9 export doesn't fill well. Audio-default assumptions matter just as much and are easier to miss: non-skippable CTV plays sound-on by default under current in-stream guidelines, which supports an audio-led opening, while most social feeds and Amazon's own Sponsored Brands video autoplay muted, which demands a visual-first, caption-supported opening that works with the sound off. A single cut built for one assumption will underperform wherever the other assumption actually applies.

File specs differ too, in ways that matter less creatively but still need checking: Prime Video's 15 Mbps minimum bitrate is well above most social platforms' requirements, and CTV's accepted duration options don't map cleanly onto typical short-form social lengths. None of these are creative decisions — they're technical requirements that fail a file outright if missed, which is why a repurposing workflow needs a checklist, not just good creative judgment.

Captioning is worth planning for at the shoot stage too, not just in post — burned-in captions designed for a muted social cut look different from platform-native caption overlays, and deciding which approach each placement needs before editing avoids a rushed caption pass added as an afterthought.

What genuinely doesn't have to change

The core message, the product story and the overall creative concept can generally travel across all three without a full reshoot. What needs adapting is the delivery mechanics — the crop, the opening few seconds, the reliance on sound — not the underlying idea. Shooting with this in mind from the start, capturing extra headroom for a vertical crop and planning a visual beat that works even muted, saves a full reshoot later and produces stronger versions of all three than editing backward from a single locked cut would.

Talent and product performances can also travel across formats without reshooting, provided the coverage captured during the shoot supports it. Directing performers to deliver key lines twice — once at a pace and framing suited to a longer CTV cut, once tighter and quicker for a quick social edit — costs very little extra time on set and avoids the awkward pacing that results from simply cutting a slower CTV performance down to fifteen seconds after the fact.

A worked example

From one shoot, build three deliverables: a 30-second 16:9 CTV master with an audio-led opening for Prime Video and Fire TV Channels, a 15-second vertical social cut with an on-screen-text-led opening that works fully muted, and a 15-30 second OLV cut that splits the difference — often horizontal like the CTV master but edited with a stronger visual hook in case the placement turns out to be a muted, skippable one. Budget the shoot to capture both horizontal and tighter, more vertical-friendly framing of the key shots, rather than shooting only wide and cropping in post, which frequently cuts off the product or the talent awkwardly.

Our own DSP book gives a concrete reason this is worth the extra production effort rather than a nice-to-have: across 27 advertisers over 31 days this summer, in-app video and in-app display ran on the same apps, the same audiences, yet display returned 6.29x against video's 1.46x. Format-specific execution, not audience targeting, explained almost all of that gap — a strong reminder that the format itself, and how well the creative fits it, carries real performance weight independent of who's being reached.

What to do when a repurposed cut underperforms

If a social cut underperforms, check first whether it's actually the CTV master reused unchanged, with a muted-first opening problem hiding underneath an otherwise fine concept — this is the single most common repurposing failure, and it's a fixable edit, not evidence the whole creative concept failed. If a CTV cut underperforms relative to social, check whether it's missing the audio lead-in that CTV's sound-on default supports, since a visual-only opening built for a muted feed leaves real opportunity on the table in an environment where sound plays automatically.

If a file gets rejected outright rather than simply underperforming, that's almost always a spec issue rather than a creative one — check bitrate and aspect ratio before anything else, since Prime Video's 15 Mbps floor in particular catches files exported at a lower bitrate for a smaller social-friendly file size.

The common mistake

The mistake is treating one export as good enough for every placement, because reshooting or re-editing for each platform feels like unnecessary cost. The technical failures — wrong aspect ratio, wrong bitrate, missed duration — get caught quickly because they reject outright. The subtler failure, a muted-first cut running sound-on or vice versa, doesn't get rejected by any spec check; it just quietly underperforms, and it's the one repurposing mistake most likely to go unnoticed for a full campaign. reMKTR plans the shoot and the edit list together from the start, specifically to avoid discovering the audio-default mismatch only after a campaign has already run.

A second version of the mistake shows up on the production side rather than the edit side: briefing a shoot only for the highest-priority format — usually CTV, since it's the most expensive placement — and treating the social and OLV versions as an afterthought to be cut from whatever footage happens to be available. Planning all three deliverables into the shot list before the shoot, not after, is what actually makes true repurposing possible rather than three unevenly-served versions of one asset.

Which one you should actually pick

This approach suits a brand producing creative for multiple Amazon DSP and social placements from a single production budget. A brand running only one placement type doesn't need the full repurposing workflow — building directly for that one format's specific requirements from the start is simpler and will outperform a version adapted from a different format's master.

Common questions

Can I use the exact same file across CTV, OLV and social?

Technically it may pass some platforms' specs, but it will underperform on at least one — the aspect ratio and, more importantly, the sound-default mismatch between non-skippable CTV and muted-first social will cost real performance even where the file is technically accepted.

What's the single most important thing to plan for during the shoot?

Framing flexibility — capture enough headroom and composition flexibility to crop both wide (16:9) and tight (vertical/square) without losing the subject, so the edit doesn't have to compromise on either version.

Does the message itself need to change between formats?

Generally not — the core story and product message can travel across formats. What needs to change is the delivery: the opening seconds, the reliance on sound, and the crop, not the underlying creative concept.

How many versions should I plan for from one shoot?

At minimum three: a 16:9 sound-on CTV master, a vertical muted-first social cut, and an OLV version built to work whether the specific placement turns out to be sound-on or muted-first.
