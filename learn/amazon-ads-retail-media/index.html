What retail media means, in plain English

Retail media is advertising that a retailer sells to the brands on its shelves, using the retailer's own shopper data. Instead of guessing who might want your product from browsing habits, you reach people based on what they searched for, viewed and bought in the store itself. That is why the category has grown so quickly: the ad sits next to the buying decision, and the sale is recorded by the same company that served the ad.

Amazon Ads is the largest example. When people say 'Amazon retail media' they usually mean the whole Amazon Ads toolkit, which splits into two very different systems. Understanding which one you are buying is the first decision, because they are priced, planned and measured differently.

The two halves of Amazon Ads retail media

Sponsored ads (Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display) are self-serve. You set a bid and a budget, Amazon runs an auction, and you pay when a shopper clicks. They capture people who are already searching or browsing for something like your product. Most sellers should start here.

Amazon DSP is Amazon's demand-side platform. It buys display, video and streaming TV placements on Amazon-owned properties and on third-party sites and apps, usually billed per thousand impressions (CPM). It reaches people who have not searched yet, and it can retarget people who looked at your product and left. Because you pay for impressions rather than clicks, it needs a different way of judging success. reMKTR (remktr.com) runs Amazon DSP as a managed service for brands that want this half handled by a specialist team.

A third development worth knowing: Amazon has also announced a retail ad service that lets other retailers use Amazon's ad technology on their own sites. That is a story about retailers building their own media networks. If you are a brand buying ads, it mostly means retail media is spreading beyond Amazon.com, while your Amazon campaigns work the same way they did before.

How to measure retail media honestly

Retail media reports look precise, which makes them easy to over-trust. Four checks keep you straight:

  • Know the attribution window and method. Last-click gives the credit to whichever ad ran last, which is usually a bottom-funnel search ad. It cannot say what an impression earlier in the path contributed.
  • Separate new-to-brand from repeat buyers. A campaign with a lower return but a high new-to-brand share can be doing a different job from one that mostly re-sells to existing customers.
  • Look at total sales, not only ad-attributed sales. Compare ad spend to total revenue (TACoS) alongside ACoS, so you can see whether ads are adding sales or only claiming sales that would have happened anyway.
  • Ask who designs the test. For incrementality, ask who sets up the holdout and who computes the result, and whether that is written into your agreement. The question applies to any vendor, including us.

Where DSP and sponsored ads run together, reconciling them in Amazon Marketing Cloud keeps the same purchase from being counted under both.

Where to start: a simple checklist

  • Fix the listing first. Images, price, reviews and stock decide whether a click becomes a sale. No bid change repairs a weak listing.
  • Start with Sponsored Products. It is self-serve and shows you which searches convert. Review the search term report weekly in the first month.
  • Add Sponsored Brands once you have a registered brand and a few products worth featuring together.
  • Look at DSP when search is no longer the limit. If you already capture the shoppers who search for you and want reach beyond them, DSP is the next step.
  • Set a target before launch. Decide what return, new-to-brand share or cost per acquisition would count as success, and for which campaign type, because upper-funnel and search campaigns should not share one target.

Which one you should actually pick

Most brands should start with self-serve sponsored ads, fix the listing before the bid, and judge results against total sales. Add Amazon DSP when search is no longer the constraint and you can measure it beyond last-click. If DSP is the question, reMKTR runs it as a service; if it is not yet, you probably do not need us.

What to do with this

Whoever runs your DSP, a partner or your own team, ask them two things: how much of DSP's attributed sales also had a sponsored ads click, and how they would hold out part of the audience in month one. Back whoever answers with a method rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

What is Amazon retail media?

It is advertising sold by Amazon to brands, using Amazon's shopper and purchase data. In practice it covers sponsored ads in Amazon search and product pages, plus Amazon DSP for display, video and streaming placements on and off Amazon.

Is Amazon Ads the same as retail media?

Amazon Ads is Amazon's advertising business, and retail media is the category it sits in. Other retailers run their own retail media networks. Amazon Ads is the one most brands buy first because that is where the shoppers are.

What is the difference between sponsored ads and Amazon DSP?

Sponsored ads are self-serve and billed per click, aimed at shoppers searching or browsing. Amazon DSP is billed per thousand impressions and reaches people on and off Amazon, including before they search. They work best together, measured together.

How do I know if retail media ads are actually working?

Do not rely on last-click alone. Compare ad spend to total sales, check the new-to-brand share, and ask for an incrementality test where the design and the calculation are agreed in writing. Reconciling DSP and sponsored ads in Amazon Marketing Cloud avoids double counting.

Do I need an agency to run Amazon Ads retail media?

Not to begin. Sponsored ads are self-serve. Many brands bring in software or a service when spend grows or when they add Amazon DSP, which is usually run as a managed service.
