What's actually available on Twitch

Twitch's Amazon DSP inventory launched on 14 December 2022 as two display formats: Stream Display, a 728×90 unit that sits directly beneath the video player, and Standard Display, 728×90 and 300×250 units on Twitch's search results pages. Both run as open-auction, non-guaranteed inventory, available globally to both managed-service and self-serve advertisers. Since Twitch was folded into Amazon's unified Streaming TV product, full-screen non-skippable video is also available on Twitch — US and Canada offer 15 or 30-second lengths, with 15, 20 or 30 seconds in the UK, Germany, Mexico and Japan.

The practical result is that a Twitch line item today can be display-only, video-only, or both, and it's worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting to whatever the campaign wizard suggests first — display and video are different formats competing for different moments of attention on the same platform. Creative specs for Twitch video follow the same general envelope Amazon publishes for its DSP video inventory broadly: 16:9, minimum 1920×1080 frame, with platforms outside Prime Video generally accepting an 8 Mbps minimum bitrate against Prime Video's own 15 Mbps floor.

Who Twitch's audience actually is

Twitch is a live-streaming platform built around gaming, but its content has broadened into general entertainment, sports commentary, music and creator-led shows. The honest read for an ecommerce brand is that it skews younger and more male than the general streaming population, and it rewards categories that already have some cultural presence in gaming and creator culture — energy drinks, snacks, gaming peripherals, apparel, tech accessories — more than it rewards categories with no natural connection to that audience, like baby products or home furnishings.

That audience is also unusually engaged rather than passively scrolling. Viewers on Twitch are typically watching a specific creator in real time, often with a live chat open alongside the stream — a very different attention state than a Prime Video viewer settling in for a two-hour film, and creative that assumes a lean-back, undistracted viewer can land flat in a context built around active participation.

A worked example

Set a $6,000 monthly test split evenly between Twitch display and Twitch video. Display CPMs on open-auction inventory generally run below premium streaming video, so allocate the display half toward volume and frequency, and the video half toward completion rate and branded search as the read. Set expectations against your account's overall streaming behavior rather than against a retargeting benchmark: across 27 advertisers in our own Amazon DSP book over 31 days this summer, connected-TV and streaming inventory overall returned 0.77x on strict last-click attribution, with the highest new-to-brand rate on the report at 56.3% — Twitch, as a discovery-first environment, sits comfortably inside that same top-of-funnel category rather than functioning like a conversion channel.

Run the test for a full month before drawing a conclusion. A gaming-audience platform like Twitch can show week-to-week swings tied to specific streams or events that a broader, always-on environment like Prime Video smooths out over a larger base of content — a single bad week is weaker evidence here than it would be elsewhere in the plan.

What to do when it goes wrong

If Twitch display delivers plenty of impressions but almost no click-through, that's closer to normal than broken — small display units below a video player are a low-intent format by design, and judging them on click-through rate the way you'd judge a search ad misreads what the format is for. Our own DSP book shows the same trap in a related format: third-party mobile in-app video across our whole account generated 48.3% of every click recorded but only 3.5% of the sales, at a $26.94 cost per acquisition against a $6.83 portfolio-wide figure — the lesson generalizes to any format where a thumb-brush or a low-friction click is easy to generate and does not reflect purchase intent.

If Twitch video isn't completing well, check creative length against what the platform's audience tolerates in a live-streaming context, where viewers are often actively engaged with chat and a stream rather than passively watching the way a Prime Video viewer might be. If the whole line item simply underperforms every other streaming supply source after a full month at real budget, that may be a genuine audience-fit signal worth acting on rather than an optimization problem to keep chasing.

The common mistake

The mistake is buying Twitch because it's available inside the same DSP seat, without asking whether the audience actually overlaps with the product. Twitch works well for the categories it naturally fits and poorly for categories it doesn't, and no amount of bid or creative optimization changes an audience-fit problem. reMKTR treats Twitch as an opt-in supply source tested against a specific category hypothesis — is this audience plausibly buying this product — rather than a default line item every streaming plan gets by default.

A second version of the same mistake is running Twitch at a token budget just to say the plan includes it. A few hundred dollars spread across a global, open-auction display format produces too few impressions to learn anything, and the honest choice is either to fund it enough to read a real result or to leave it out of the plan until there's budget to test it properly.

Which one you should actually pick

Twitch suits brands whose product has genuine relevance to gaming, esports or creator culture, and who are willing to test both display and video formats separately, at a real budget, to find which one their audience actually responds to. A brand with no natural connection to that audience is better served putting the budget toward Prime Video or Fire TV Channels first, where the reach is broader and less culturally specific, and returning to Twitch once the rest of the plan is already working.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Then ask each vendor on your list what they would do about it in week one, and see who answers with a process rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

Is Twitch inventory guaranteed or open-auction?

Twitch's original display formats launched as open-auction, non-guaranteed inventory. Confirm current terms for video formats in your DSP console, since availability and deal structure can change as the product evolves.

What ecommerce categories actually work on Twitch?

Categories with a natural connection to gaming and creator culture — energy drinks, snacks, gaming peripherals, apparel, tech accessories — tend to perform better than categories with no cultural link to the audience, like home goods or baby products. Test before committing a large budget either way.

Can I run Twitch display and Twitch video in one campaign?

Yes, both are available as Amazon DSP supply sources, though running them as separate line items makes it easier to tell which format is actually driving results rather than reading a blended average.

Does Twitch inventory cost more or less than Prime Video?

Amazon doesn't publish a rate card for either, but open-auction display inventory on Twitch generally trades at lower CPMs than premium full-screen video on Prime Video's original programming — treat any specific figure as auction-driven and current only to the day you check it.

How much budget do I need to properly test Twitch?

There's no official minimum specific to Twitch, but a budget too small to generate a statistically meaningful number of impressions on an open-auction format will produce noise rather than a usable read — a few thousand dollars over a full month is a more honest test than a few hundred dollars spread thin.

Is Twitch worth testing if my product has no obvious gaming connection?

It can still be worth a small, deliberate test rather than a large default allocation — Twitch's audience has broadened well beyond gaming into general entertainment and creator content. Treat the first month as a genuine test with a clear stop-loss rather than an assumption either way.
