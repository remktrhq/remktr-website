Freevee Advertising Explained

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1388 words · Written against what currently ranked for “Freevee advertising explained”
The short answer

Freevee, Amazon's free ad-supported streaming brand, no longer exists as a standalone product. Amazon began winding it down in 2024, pulled the standalone app from app stores by August 2025, and folded its entire content library into Prime Video's ad-supported "Watch for Free" section by early September 2025. You cannot buy "Freevee" as a separate destination today — that inventory is now part of Prime Video's ad-supported supply.

What happened to Freevee, in order

Freevee started as IMDb TV, rebranded to Amazon Freevee, and operated as Amazon's standalone free, ad-supported streaming service. The retirement began after Prime Video started running ads by default in January 2024, which made a second, separate ad-supported brand redundant — Amazon was effectively running two ad-supported streaming products doing overlapping jobs. Amazon announced consolidation plans in 2024, confirmed the standalone app would shut down in a July 2025 announcement, and the app was pulled from app stores by August 2025. By 3 September 2025, Freevee's full catalog — including originals like Jury Duty — had moved into a dedicated "Watch for Free" section inside Prime Video, still free to anyone with an Amazon account, still carrying ads.

None of this is a small footnote. It's the same pattern that's played out repeatedly in this category — a brand or platform gets absorbed into a larger sibling product, and every guide written before the absorption keeps circulating with outdated instructions for months or years afterward. Freevee's timeline is worth having in order specifically because so much of what still ranks for "Freevee advertising" was written before September 2025 and never updated.

Why this still matters to an advertiser today

Older guides — and a fair number of current ones — still describe Freevee as something you buy separately from Prime Video. That's out of date. The consumer-facing brand is gone, but the ad-supported content it represented did not disappear; it's simply inside Prime Video's inventory now, generally as part of the same free ad-supported experience described elsewhere as "Amazon Streaming TV" supply. Some Amazon Ads documentation and delivery reporting has continued to reference Freevee by name at the supply-source level even after the consumer brand retired, which is exactly the kind of detail that's easy to get wrong in either direction — assuming it's a dead buy, or assuming it's still a separate live product with its own account.

Why it's worth its own line item anyway

Even folded into Prime Video, ad-supported "free with ads" content and Prime Video's premium subscription-tier content are not the same audience or the same viewing context, and our own internal practice treats them accordingly: Prime Video's premium ad inventory and its free ad-supported channel inventory behave differently and shouldn't sit in one blended line item. A viewer choosing free, ad-supported content specifically is a different signal than a Prime subscriber watching an ad-supported show inside their existing subscription — worth separating in your reporting even if the buying mechanics have merged into one product.

This isn't a hypothetical distinction. A shopper actively choosing free, ad-supported content over a paid alternative is telling you something about price sensitivity that a Prime subscriber, who has already committed to a paid membership, is not. For a value-priced or promotionally-driven product, that's a genuinely useful signal to isolate rather than average away.

A worked example

If you're setting up a Prime Video buy today and want to isolate what used to be Freevee's audience, look for a free ad-supported or "Watch for Free" targeting option inside your Prime Video supply-source settings rather than searching for a "Freevee" line item that no longer exists as such. Budget it the way you'd budget any ad-supported, broad-reach inventory — closer to the lower end of the reported $20–$50 premium streaming CPM range than the top, since free ad-supported content generally reaches a more price-sensitive supply pool than Prime Video's flagship originals.

On a $5,000 monthly test at a representative $18 CPM, that's roughly 278,000 impressions reaching a specifically ad-tolerant, cost-conscious audience segment — a genuinely different targeting proposition than the same $5,000 spent chasing Prime Video's premium originals, even though both now technically sit inside the same product.

What to do when it goes wrong

If a media plan or a vendor proposal still lists "Freevee" as a distinct line item with its own separate budget, that's worth a direct question about whether they mean Prime Video's current free ad-supported inventory or are simply working from an outdated template. If your own delivery reporting shows a "Freevee" supply-source tag, treat it as legacy naming for the same content pool rather than evidence of a separate buyable product — check current terminology in your DSP console rather than assuming either the old or new name is definitively correct without confirming.

The common mistake

The mistake is one of two things: either avoiding this inventory because a brand or a media plan template still calls it "Freevee" and nobody's sure if it still exists, or treating it as identical to premium Prime Video content and blending the two into one line item that hides which audience is actually converting. Both come from the same root cause — not updating a mental model after a brand retirement.

The broader lesson is worth stating plainly: streaming TV inventory has consolidated repeatedly over the last few years, and a media plan built once and never revisited will accumulate exactly this kind of stale reference. reMKTR runs 109 live Amazon DSP advertiser seats and keeps free ad-supported inventory as its own reporting line inside Prime Video specifically so a brand-name change from years ago doesn't quietly corrupt how the data gets read today, and revisits the current state of every supply source at least once a quarter rather than trusting a plan built on last year's product names.

Which one you should actually pick

This inventory suits any brand already running Prime Video ads who wants broad, cost-efficient reach and is willing to isolate the free ad-supported audience from Prime Video's premium content as its own reporting line. It does not suit a media plan that still budgets for it as a standalone product with its own account — that line item should simply be folded into a current Prime Video plan and relabelled accordingly.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Then ask each vendor on your list what they would do about it in week one, and see who answers with a process rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

Can I still buy Freevee as a separate product from Amazon Ads?

No. Freevee's standalone app and brand were retired, with the app pulled from stores by August 2025 and its content folded into Prime Video's free ad-supported section by September 2025. What you can buy today is Prime Video's ad-supported inventory, which includes that content.

Do I need a Prime subscription to watch what used to be Freevee content?

No — that content remains free to anyone with an Amazon account, with ads included, inside Prime Video's dedicated free section. No Prime membership is required to access it.

Why do some reports still show 'Freevee' as a line item?

Delivery reporting and some Amazon Ads documentation have continued to use Freevee as a supply-source label even after the consumer brand retired. Treat it as legacy naming for the same content pool, not evidence of a separate live product.

Should free ad-supported Prime Video inventory be measured differently than premium Prime Video content?

We think so, and treat it as its own reporting line in our own accounts — a viewer choosing free, ad-supported content is a different signal than a paying subscriber encountering an ad inside their existing Prime membership, even though both now sit inside the same Prime Video product.

Is IMDb TV the same thing as Freevee?

Yes — Freevee was IMDb TV rebranded, before Freevee itself was later retired as a standalone brand and folded into Prime Video. Both names describe the same lineage of content, now sitting inside Prime Video's free ad-supported section.
