Amazon Sponsored Display Ads: What They Are and When to Use Them

Updated November 3, 2026 · By Ken Freeman · 1468 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon sponsored display ads”
The short answer

Amazon Sponsored Display is a self-serve ad format that puts your product in front of shoppers on Amazon product pages and across Amazon-owned and third-party sites and apps. You target by product, category or audience, and you can reach shoppers who viewed your listing but did not buy. Amazon DSP is the larger, programmatic step up.

What Sponsored Display actually is

Amazon sells three self-serve sponsored formats: Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display. The first two mostly capture shoppers who are already searching. Sponsored Display is the one built around the shopper rather than the search term. You choose who or what you want to appear next to, and Amazon decides where the ad shows.

Placements include product detail pages, the areas next to and below search results, and Amazon-owned and third-party sites and apps. Creative is assembled for you from your listing, so you do not need to upload a banner to get started. Check Amazon's own advertising pages for the current eligibility rules, because they have changed over the years and differ for sellers and vendors.

Two things make it different from the search formats. First, you can retarget: show an ad to shoppers who viewed your product or similar ones and left. Second, you can place your product on a competitor's detail page or in a category rather than waiting for a keyword to trigger it.

How targeting works

Sponsored Display gives you two families of targeting. Think of them as answering two different questions.

  • Contextual targeting answers: what is the shopper looking at right now? You pick specific products or categories, and your ad appears on those pages. It suits conquesting against a competitor listing or defending your own detail pages.
  • Audience targeting answers: who is this shopper? Options include shoppers who viewed your products or similar ones (the retargeting use), and Amazon audience segments built from shopping and browsing signals. It suits bringing back people who showed interest.

A practical way to start: run one campaign that retargets your own product views, and a separate one that targets your own competing-but-related ASINs on detail pages. Keep them separate so the reports tell you which is working. Mixed campaigns blur that answer.

How bidding and cost work

Sponsored Display is auction based. Depending on the campaign goal you pick, you are billed per click or per thousand viewable impressions (vCPM). Click billing is the familiar model from Sponsored Products. vCPM suits awareness goals, where you want the ad seen and are not counting on a click.

The question that matters is not the CPC itself, it is what the CPC can afford. Here is a worked example, not a quote and not a reMKTR result. Say a product sells for $30 and leaves $12 after product cost and Amazon fees, before ads. If clicks cost $0.90 and 8% of clicks buy, each order costs $0.90 divided by 0.08, which is $11.25 in ads. That leaves $0.75 per order, so it works, but barely. Your break-even bid is the $12 left times the 8% conversion rate, which is $0.96 a click. Bid above that and every order loses money.

Retargeting audiences usually convert better than cold category targeting, because the shopper has already seen the product. That is a reason to give the two types different bids, not a reason to assume either will work for your listing.

Sponsored Display vs Amazon DSP

The most common question behind this keyword is whether Sponsored Display is the same thing as Amazon DSP. It is not. They share some placements and some audience ideas, but they are bought differently and measured differently.

  • Sponsored Display is self-serve, runs from the same console as your other sponsored ads, and is built for sellers and vendors advertising products they sell on Amazon.
  • Amazon DSP is Amazon's programmatic platform. It buys display, video and streaming TV on Amazon-owned surfaces and on third-party supply, and it is usually run by an agency or managed service once there is a media plan and a measurement question to answer.

A checklist before you launch

Use this before you spend the first dollar:

  • Know your break-even. Work out what is left per order before ads, and the conversion rate you honestly expect, as in the example above.
  • Check your listing first. If shoppers click and do not buy, a better bid will not fix images, price or reviews.
  • Separate campaigns by intent. One for your own product views, one for competitor and category targeting.
  • Set a budget you can afford to learn with, and give each campaign enough days to collect real data before you change it.
  • Review where the ad is showing. Look at the targeting and placement reports and prune what is not earning.
  • Judge it fairly. A retargeting ad often shows up after a shopper first met your brand elsewhere, so last-click ACoS undersells it. Compare total sales and branded search over time, not only the campaign line.

Common mistakes

The first is treating Sponsored Display like Sponsored Products and expecting the same ACoS. It sits earlier in the shopper's path, so it is often judged by what it helps close, not only by what it closes itself.

The second is bidding on category targets without a floor. Broad categories can spend quickly on shoppers with little intent. Start with specific products you have a reason to target.

The third is never looking at the reports. Every ad type teaches you something about your listing and your market, but only if you read the placement and targeting data and act on it.

Which one you should actually pick

Sponsored Display suits sellers and vendors who already run search ads and want to retarget interested shoppers or sit on relevant product pages, with a bid held below break-even. Amazon DSP suits brands that need video, streaming or a wider audience plan with proper measurement. Start with the first, and move to the second when the plan needs it.

What to do with this

Whoever runs your DSP, a partner or your own team, ask them two things: how much of DSP's attributed sales also had a sponsored ads click, and how they would hold out part of the audience in month one. Back whoever answers with a method rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

What are Amazon Sponsored Display ads?

They are self-serve ads that target shoppers by product, category or audience, including people who viewed your product and did not buy. They appear on Amazon product pages and on Amazon-owned and third-party sites and apps.

Are Sponsored Display ads worth it?

They can be, if the economics work. Work out what is left per order before ads, then compare it with your cost per click divided by your conversion rate. If the ad cost per order is below what is left, the campaign can pay. Retargeting your own product views is a sensible first test.

What is the difference between Sponsored Display and Amazon DSP?

Sponsored Display is self-serve and built for products you sell on Amazon. Amazon DSP is a programmatic platform with broader inventory including video and streaming TV, and it is usually run as a managed service.

How much do Sponsored Display ads cost?

It is an auction, so there is no fixed price. You pay per click or per thousand viewable impressions depending on the goal you choose. Set a bid no higher than your break-even bid, which is what is left per order times your conversion rate.

Can I use Sponsored Display to retarget shoppers?

Yes. Audience targeting lets you show ads to shoppers who viewed your products or similar ones. Check Amazon's advertising pages for the current eligibility and options for your account type.
