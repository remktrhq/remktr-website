What Amazon Product Display Ads Are

People search for "Amazon product display ads" because Amazon has used that name for display formats that put a product, a price and a button in front of shoppers. The format you can switch on yourself today is Sponsored Display. It sits alongside Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands in the same Amazon Ads console.

The difference from the other two is where the ad can appear and who it is aimed at. Sponsored Products mostly answers a shopper who is already searching. A display ad can reach a shopper on a competing product page, on a category page, or after they have left Amazon for another site. That makes it a tool for people who have not typed your keyword yet, or who looked and did not buy.

The ad itself is built from your listing: the product image, the price, the rating and a link to the detail page. You do not need a design team to run one, which is the main reason it is the usual first step into display.

Where the Ads Show and Who They Reach

Product display ads can run in a few places. Exact placements change as Amazon updates its console, so check the current Amazon Ads help pages before you plan around one.

  • On product detail pages, including pages for other brands' products when you target them.
  • In and around search results, where the ad sits near organic and sponsored listings.
  • On category and browse pages, when you target a category instead of a single product.
  • Off Amazon, on third-party sites and apps, when the campaign uses audiences.

The audience options are what make display different from keyword ads. You can aim at shoppers who viewed your product or similar products, at people who bought from you before, or at interest-based groups. The first two are remarketing: you are paying to bring back someone who already showed interest.

How Targeting and Bidding Work

A Sponsored Display campaign starts with a choice between two broad approaches.

  • Product and category targeting. You pick the products or categories whose pages should carry your ad. This is the way to put your product next to a competitor's.
  • Audience targeting. You pick a group of shoppers, such as people who viewed your listing without buying. The ad follows them.

You are billed per click (CPC) or per thousand viewable impressions (vCPM), depending on the campaign goal you choose. Click billing suits a conversion goal, where you only pay when someone shows interest. Viewable-impression billing suits an awareness goal, where you pay for being seen.

The bid you can afford comes from your margin, not from a rule of thumb. Here is a worked example with made-up numbers: a $40 product that leaves $14 before ad costs can spend up to $14 per order. If about 10% of clicks become orders, the most you can bid per click and still break even is $14 x 10% = $1.40. A remarketing audience often converts better than a cold one, so it can justify a higher bid. A competitor-page placement often converts worse, so it needs a lower one. Set a separate target for each, not one blended number.

Product Display Ads vs Sponsored Products vs DSP

Most sellers run these in a sensible order. Start with Sponsored Products, add Sponsored Display for remarketing and competitor pages, and move to Amazon DSP when you want reach beyond what the self-serve tools give you.

Amazon DSP is programmatic display, video and streaming bought by impression. It reaches Amazon-owned surfaces and third-party sites, and it supports the same kinds of audiences with more control over inventory and frequency. It is also where measurement gets harder, because a display impression often helps a sale that a search ad then gets credit for.

To show what a larger display programme looks like, here are our own figures. Across 30 advertisers we managed on Amazon DSP in July 2026, the portfolio ran 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM and a blended $1.42 cost per click, with 20.1% of attributed purchases coming from shoppers new to the brand. Return on ad spend across that portfolio was 6.04x. That is one mixed portfolio in a single month, not a promise of what your account will do.

A Checklist Before You Launch

Use this list before you turn on your first display campaign.

  • Fix the listing first. A display ad sends traffic to your detail page. If the images, price or reviews are weak, more traffic only costs more.
  • Work out your break-even bid from your margin and conversion rate, using the same method as the example above.
  • Separate remarketing from cold targeting. They behave differently and should have their own campaigns and budgets.
  • Exclude your own products from product targeting so you do not bid against yourself.
  • Pick one goal per campaign. Conversion and awareness need different bids and different yardsticks.
  • Check results weekly at first. Early bids are a guess until real data comes in.

When you read results, remember that display often assists a sale that another ad closes. Judging it only by the last click it received will usually make it look weaker than it is. A holdout test, or a view of display and search together in Amazon Marketing Cloud, gives a fairer answer.

Which one you should actually pick

Sponsored Display is the practical way to run Amazon product display ads: self-serve, low-risk, good for remarketing and competitor pages. Amazon DSP suits brands that want wider reach and will measure it properly. Most sellers should start with Sponsored Products, add Sponsored Display, and consider DSP when display becomes the question.

Common questions

What are Amazon product display ads?

They are display ads that feature a product on Amazon product pages, category pages, search results and other sites. The self-serve version today is Sponsored Display, set up in the Amazon Ads console.

Are Amazon product display ads the same as Sponsored Display?

For most sellers, yes. Amazon used the older name for earlier display formats, and Sponsored Display is the self-serve product you will find in the console now. Check Amazon's current help pages for the exact format names.

How much do Amazon display ads cost?

You set the budget and the bid. Sponsored Display is billed per click or per thousand viewable impressions, depending on the goal you choose. Work out a break-even bid from your own margin and conversion rate before you start.

Can I show my product on a competitor's page?

Yes. Product targeting lets you choose the product pages where your ad appears. Expect it to convert differently from remarketing, so give it its own campaign and its own target.

When should I move from Sponsored Display to Amazon DSP?

When you want reach beyond what self-serve display gives you, and you are set up to measure it with a holdout or in Amazon Marketing Cloud rather than by last click. reMKTR runs Amazon DSP as a service for brands at that point.
