Amazon Attribution for Shopify: What It Can and Cannot Track

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1227 words · Written against what currently ranked for “Amazon Attribution for Shopify: what it can and cannot track”
The short answer

Amazon Attribution can track clicks, detail page views, add-to-cart actions and purchases that happen on Amazon, resulting from a tagged link on Google, Meta, email or any off-Amazon channel — including a link on your Shopify site pointed at your Amazon listing. It cannot track a conversion that completes entirely on Shopify, because Amazon Attribution measures Amazon-side activity only.

What Amazon Attribution is actually built to measure

Amazon's own documentation describes the tool's purpose plainly: it measures how marketing channels beyond Amazon influence purchasing activity and sales on Amazon. It works by generating unique tracking tags you attach to external marketing links — on Google, Meta, email, wherever — and when a shopper clicks a tagged link and takes an action on Amazon, that action is captured and reported: clicks, product detail page views, add-to-cart, purchases, sales, and new-to-brand status, all measured against Amazon Attribution's own 14-day last-touch model.

The boundary that matters for a Shopify-and-Amazon brand

The scope is Amazon-side activity, full stop. If you tag a Shopify banner or email pointing a customer to your Amazon listing, Amazon Attribution will correctly measure everything that happens once they land on Amazon — a genuinely useful tool for understanding whether your own site's traffic is driving Amazon sales. What it cannot do is the reverse: measure a conversion that happens on your Shopify checkout itself, whether that traffic came from a DSP link-out campaign, a Google ad, or anywhere else. Amazon Attribution's tracking tags and reporting infrastructure simply don't extend into a non-Amazon checkout flow — that measurement gap is structural, not a configuration you can turn on.

A worked example of both directions

Direction one: you run a Klaviyo email campaign to your existing Shopify customer list, with a tagged Amazon Attribution link pointed at your Amazon listing, promoting a bundle only available there. Amazon Attribution will show you exactly how many of those email clicks resulted in an Amazon detail page view, add-to-cart, and purchase — genuinely useful, native measurement.

Direction two: you run an Amazon DSP link-out campaign sending clicks to your Shopify checkout. Amazon's own DSP reporting shows you the clicks and the spend. Amazon Attribution shows you nothing about what happened after that click, because the resulting conversion — if any — happened entirely on Shopify, outside Amazon's measurement scope. You need Shopify's own analytics, a Meta or Google-style conversion pixel on your own site, or a server-side event feed to see that second number at all.

The common mistake, including ours

The mistake is assuming Amazon Attribution is symmetric — that because it measures off-Amazon-to-Amazon traffic, it must also measure Amazon-to-off-Amazon traffic the same way. It doesn't, and the two directions require completely different tools. We've fielded a client question asking why an Amazon Attribution report showed no conversions for a link-out campaign that their own Shopify dashboard confirmed was converting reasonably well — the honest answer was that Amazon Attribution was never going to show that number, because it wasn't built to measure a Shopify checkout at all, in either direction.

What to use instead for the direction Amazon Attribution can't cover

For measuring conversions on your own site from any traffic source, including Amazon DSP link-out campaigns, rely on your Shopify store's native analytics, a properly configured conversion pixel, or a server-side event feed depending on your setup and any browser tracking restrictions in play. Build the join between Amazon's own spend reporting (available from the DSP console for a link-out campaign) and your Shopify conversion data using a consistent UTM parameter or dedicated tracking link, checked regularly rather than assumed to be working correctly indefinitely.

A note on Amazon Marketing Cloud, since it's often confused with Amazon Attribution here

Amazon Marketing Cloud can incorporate first-party data you deliberately upload — including, in principle, your own Shopify conversion events — and query it alongside Amazon's ad signals. That's a genuinely different capability from Amazon Attribution, and it's not automatic: AMC has no native visibility into Shopify either, until you build and maintain the upload pipeline yourself. Don't assume having AMC access solves the Shopify measurement gap without deliberately setting up that data bridge.

Why this distinction matters more as link-out spend grows

A brand running a small link-out test can get away with loose measurement discipline because the stakes are low. As link-out spend scales, the gap between what Amazon Attribution reports (nothing, for the Shopify-side conversion) and what actually happened becomes a real blind spot in budget decisions if the bridge to Shopify analytics isn't built and maintained properly. Treat the measurement bridge as part of the campaign infrastructure, built before spend scales, not an afterthought added once someone asks why the numbers don't add up.

The failure mode to watch for specifically is a pixel or tracking convention that quietly breaks — a site redesign, a new checkout provider, a change to consent management that blocks a script — while Amazon's own DSP reporting keeps showing spend and clicks as if nothing changed. Because Amazon Attribution was never reporting the Shopify-side conversion anyway, there's no obvious signal that the bridge has broken; it just silently stops producing a number nobody was watching closely enough to notice was missing.

Common questions

Can Amazon Attribution track a sale on my Shopify site?

No — Amazon Attribution measures off-Amazon marketing channels' impact on activity that happens on Amazon. It has no visibility into a conversion that completes on a non-Amazon checkout.

Can I use Amazon Attribution to track my Shopify email campaigns driving Amazon sales?

Yes — that's exactly the use case Amazon Attribution is built for. Tag your email links to your Amazon listing and it will report the resulting clicks, page views and purchases on Amazon.

What should I use to measure conversions from a DSP link-out campaign?

Your own site's analytics, a conversion pixel, or a server-side event feed — Amazon's reporting stack only shows the click and spend for a link-out campaign, not what happened after the click landed on your site.

Is there any way to get Amazon to natively bridge my Shopify conversion data?

Not through Amazon Attribution directly. You can bring your own first-party conversion data into Amazon Marketing Cloud as a deliberate upload, which enables some cross-referencing, but it's a manual pipeline you build, not an automatic bridge.
