Amazon Audiences Applied to Your Own Site Traffic

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1173 words · Written against what currently ranked for “Amazon audiences applied to your own site traffic”
The short answer

Amazon audiences can't be directly applied to identify or retarget visitors already on your own site — that's not how the signal works. What is possible: build an Amazon-signal-based audience in AMC or DSP, then reach that same audience through Amazon DSP media buying, which can include serving ads that link to your own site. The audience travels with the media buy, not onto your website's own targeting stack.

Clarifying what this question is actually asking

This phrase gets searched with two different intents worth separating. One version asks: can I take Amazon's shopping-signal audience data and apply it inside my own website's targeting or personalisation tools, the way you might import a Meta custom audience into an email platform. The honest answer is no — Amazon's shopping signals are pseudonymized and stay inside Amazon's own systems; there's no export mechanism that hands you a usable audience list to plug into your own site's tooling. The other version asks: can I use Amazon's audience data to reach people through media that lands on my own site — and that one is genuinely possible, through Amazon DSP link-out campaigns.

What's actually possible: audience-informed link-out campaigns

Build an audience inside Amazon DSP or Amazon Marketing Cloud — based on Amazon shopping behaviour, category browsing, cart abandonment, or a lookalike model — and use Amazon DSP to serve display or video ads to that audience wherever DSP delivers, including link-out creative pointed at your own site. The audience definition and targeting happen entirely within Amazon's systems; what changes is only the landing page the resulting click goes to. That's meaningfully different from "applying Amazon audiences to my site traffic" in the sense of retargeting people already browsing your site — it's targeting people based on Amazon signal, then sending some of them to your site via the ad itself.

The reverse direction: bringing your own site's data into Amazon's systems

The other half of this question — using your own site's visitor data to build or refine an Amazon-side audience — is possible, but only through a deliberate upload. Amazon Marketing Cloud accepts first-party data uploads, including hashed customer lists, which can then be used to build lookalike audiences or exclusion lists for DSP targeting. That's a real capability, but it requires you to bring the data in explicitly; there's no automatic sync between your Shopify visitor list and Amazon's audience-building tools.

A worked example of the full loop

Say you upload your existing Shopify customer email list (hashed, per AMC's data requirements) into Amazon Marketing Cloud. You build a lookalike audience from that seed inside DSP, targeting people with similar Amazon shopping signals to your existing best customers. You run a DSP link-out campaign to that lookalike audience, pointed at your Shopify site. The resulting site traffic then generates its own conversion data — which, per the measurement gap covered elsewhere on this site, Amazon Attribution won't natively see, so you'd need your own site analytics to close that final loop and confirm the lookalike audience actually converted well.

The common mistake, including ours

The mistake is assuming a phrase like "apply Amazon audiences to my site" describes a single, simple integration, when it actually spans several separate capabilities — audience building inside Amazon's systems, media targeting through DSP, an optional first-party data upload, and separate site-side measurement — each requiring its own setup. We've had a client ask for this as if it were a one-click feature to enable, and the honest response was walking through which specific piece of the loop they actually wanted, since the full chain isn't a single toggle anywhere in Amazon's tools.

Why the confusion is understandable, not a sign of a bad question

The confusion behind this search term is genuinely reasonable, not a knowledge gap worth feeling bad about — most advertising platforms blur the line between "audience" and "data" in their marketing language, using the same word to describe a targeting capability and an exportable asset interchangeably. Amazon's audience infrastructure is a targeting capability, not a data product you own and can move elsewhere, which is a meaningfully different thing from how a CRM export or a Meta custom audience list works, and it's worth stating plainly rather than assuming the reader already knows the distinction.

What to actually set up, depending on what you're trying to do

If the goal is reaching Amazon-signal audiences with ads that land on your own site, build the audience in DSP or AMC and run a link-out campaign — that's straightforwardly available. If the goal is improving Amazon-side targeting using your own customer knowledge, build the first-party upload pipeline into AMC deliberately. If the goal is retargeting your own site's actual visitors, that's a separate capability entirely, handled through your own site's pixel and a platform like Meta or Google, not through Amazon's audience infrastructure at all.

Common questions

Can I export Amazon's shopping audience data to use in my own marketing tools?

No — Amazon's shopping signals are pseudonymized and remain inside Amazon's own systems. There's no export mechanism that hands you a usable audience list for external tools.

Can I upload my own customer data to improve Amazon DSP targeting?

Yes — Amazon Marketing Cloud accepts first-party data uploads, like hashed customer lists, which can be used to build lookalike audiences or exclusions for DSP targeting.

Does Amazon audience targeting work for retargeting my own site's actual visitors?

No — retargeting people currently browsing your own site is handled through your own site's pixel and platforms like Meta or Google, not through Amazon's audience infrastructure.

Will I be able to measure how well an Amazon-audience link-out campaign performs on my site?

Only if you set up your own site's conversion tracking — Amazon's own reporting tools don't natively measure conversions completing on a non-Amazon site.
