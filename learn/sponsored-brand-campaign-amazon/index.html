What a Sponsored Brand campaign actually is

Sponsored Brands is Amazon's format for showing up as a brand, not just a product. Instead of one listing in the search results, you get your logo, a headline you write, and a group of products — or a video — placed at the top of search, on product pages, or on the Amazon home page. Click it, and the shopper lands on your Brand Store, a specific product page, or a custom landing page you build.

It's built for the top and middle of the funnel: getting found, getting considered, and getting shoppers into your Store where they can see the rest of your catalog. Only sellers enrolled in Brand Registry, vendors, and agencies acting on their behalf can run it. If you're not enrolled, this format isn't available to you yet — that's the first thing to check if you can't find it in the console.

The three formats, and which one to pick

Sponsored Brands isn't one ad — it's three creative formats sharing one campaign type. A cookware brand the size of HexClad might run a product collection ad to move a seasonal set, while a brand with a well-built-out Store might lean on Store spotlight to pull shoppers deeper into specific subpages. Video runs in its own auction, which matters more than it sounds like — see the pricing section below.

What it costs, and the budget math

Sponsored Brands charges on cost per click or cost per 1,000 viewable impressions (vCPM), your choice at setup. There's no minimum spend requirement, though Amazon suggests budgeting at least $10 a day so your ads run through the full day instead of exhausting the budget by mid-morning.

Here's where a lot of planning goes wrong: people benchmark against a single CPC number pulled from a listicle — often around $0.41 — and set budgets expecting that rate everywhere. That figure is real, but it's usually the online-video placement only, not a blended number across formats. Across a book of 30 advertisers we manage, running Sponsored Brands alongside DSP, the blended cost per click was $1.42, on 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM. Different placements, different auctions, different price.

So do the math with your actual placement mix, not the headline number. A $50 daily budget at a $1.42 blended CPC buys roughly 35 clicks a day. If your Store or landing page converts at 10%, that's three to four orders a day from this campaign alone, before you count what those shoppers buy elsewhere in your catalog on the same visit.

Setting one up: targeting, creative, and the Store link

You target by keyword, category, or specific product — the same three options as Sponsored Products, applied to a brand-level ad instead of a single listing. Creative requirements are stricter than they look: your logo needs to be the actual registered brand logo, your headline has a character limit and can't make claims you can't back up, and video needs to run 6 to 45 seconds and autoplay cleanly without sound.

Store spotlight has a hard eligibility rule people miss: your Brand Store needs at least three subpages, each with a distinct product, before the format is even available to select. Ridge or Beardbrand, or any brand with a Store built around distinct product lines, would hit this requirement without noticing. A brand with a single-page Store won't see the option at all — that's not a bug, it's the gate.

The mistakes that actually cost money

  • Benchmarking on the wrong CPC. Treating a video-only figure like $0.41 as your expected blended rate, then panicking when your real number comes in over $1.
  • Linking to a broken destination. Sending traffic to a Store page that isn't finished, or a landing page that doesn't match the ad's promise.
  • Skipping video because it feels like extra competition. It runs in its own auction — it doesn't cannibalize your other Sponsored Brands campaigns' impressions.
  • Reading attributed ROAS as proven incrementality. This one we've made ourselves. The in-console number tells you a sale happened after a click. It doesn't tell you the sale wouldn't have happened anyway.

What the ROAS number in your dashboard isn't telling you

Sponsored Brands reporting will hand you a clean ROAS, an ACOS, and a click-through rate. What it won't tell you is whether that sale was incremental — whether the shopper would have bought anyway, found you through Sponsored Products, or converted from a DSP display impression they saw the day before. Last-click attribution assigns the whole sale to whichever ad got clicked last. It was never built to answer the incrementality question, and no amount of dashboard refreshing fixes that.

The only way to actually answer it is a holdout test or a matched control group, and reconciling spend and sales across formats in one place — Amazon Marketing Cloud — instead of adding up two systems that are each taking credit for the same purchase. Across one 30-advertiser book we run this way, combining DSP and sponsored formats and measuring the whole thing rather than the best-performing line item, the numbers were a 6.04x ROAS, a $5.49 blended cost per acquisition across 57,137 attributed purchases, and 20.1% of those purchases from shoppers new to the brand. None of that comes from a single campaign's self-reported ROAS — it comes from reconciling the whole account.

Common questions

Do I need Brand Registry to run Sponsored Brands?

Yes. Sponsored Brands is only available to sellers enrolled in Brand Registry, along with vendors and agencies managing accounts on their behalf. If the format isn't showing up in your console, enrollment is the first thing to check.

What's the difference between Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Products?

Sponsored Products advertises one listing and looks like an organic search result. Sponsored Brands advertises the brand itself — your logo, a headline, and a group of products or a video — and is built to send traffic to your Store, not just a single product page.

Is there a minimum daily budget for Sponsored Brands?

No hard minimum exists, but Amazon recommends at least $10 a day so the ad has enough budget to keep running through the day instead of stopping early. Below that, you'll likely see inconsistent delivery rather than a rejected campaign.

Why can't I select the Store spotlight format?

Store spotlight requires your Brand Store to have at least three subpages, each featuring a distinct product. If your Store is a single page, the format won't appear as an option until you build it out.

Does a high ROAS in Sponsored Brands mean the campaign is working?

It means sales happened after clicks — it doesn't mean those sales wouldn't have happened without the ad. Last-click attribution can't separate incremental sales from sales that were coming anyway. A holdout test or a matched control group is the only way to answer that question directly.
