Which Streaming Services Can Amazon DSP Actually Reach

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1358 words · Written against what currently ranked for “Which streaming services can Amazon DSP actually reach”
The short answer

Amazon DSP directly buys Amazon's own inventory (Prime Video, Fire TV Channels, Twitch), Disney's Disney+, ESPN and Hulu since June 2025, and Netflix since Q4 2025. A separate June 2025 Roku partnership extends addressability — identity resolution and frequency capping, not necessarily a new direct-buy channel — across roughly 80 million US CTV households on Roku-connected apps. Those two categories, buyable and addressable, are worth keeping distinct.

Directly buyable: the confirmed list

Three categories are unambiguous. Amazon's own inventory — Prime Video, Fire TV Channels' 900+ ad-supported channels, Twitch's display and video formats, and Amazon's free ad-supported content, previously branded Freevee — is native to Amazon DSP with no integration required. Disney's Disney+, ESPN and Hulu arrived through the DRAX integration announced 17 June 2025, reaching full US access by Q3 2025, with Disney's Magic Words contextual targeting and eventual Disney Select data layered on. Netflix arrived through a separate partnership announced 10 September 2025, live from Q4 2025 across the eleven markets Amazon's announcement names — the US, UK, France, Spain, Mexico, Canada, Japan, Brazil, Italy, Germany and Australia. All three categories are directly buyable — you select them as a supply source and run a campaign against them the same way you would any other Amazon DSP line item.

Across 27 advertisers in our own DSP book over a 31-day window this summer, delivery actually touched 134 distinct named supply sources — evidence that the buyable list is considerably longer and more fragmented than any three-category summary, this one included, fully captures. Treat lists like this one as a starting map, not an exhaustive one, and confirm the current full set directly in your own console before final budget decisions.

Addressable but not necessarily buyable: the Roku distinction

The Roku partnership, also announced in June 2025, is a different kind of integration and worth reading carefully rather than folding into the same bucket as Disney and Netflix. It extends Amazon's identity resolution — deterministic recognition of a logged-in household — across an estimated 80 million US CTV households on Roku-connected devices, described publicly as enhancing addressability across apps including The Roku Channel, Prime Video and other services running on Roku hardware, and mentioning identity-level benefits touching Disney, Fox, Paramount+, Pluto TV, Tubi and WarnerBros Discovery content within that footprint. What's specifically confirmed is improved frequency capping, reduced redundant exposure, and consistent identity across that device footprint — not a blanket claim that every named publisher's inventory became newly buyable through Amazon DSP as a direct result. Check current documentation before building a plan that assumes Paramount+ or Pluto TV inventory is purchasable the same way Disney+ or Netflix inventory is.

A useful habit here is to ask any partner or vendor claiming access to a specific platform to point to where it's selectable in the console, rather than accepting a press-release-level claim at face value — a request that costs nothing and quickly separates confirmed access from optimistic framing.

A worked example of the practical difference

Say a plan wants to reach households on Hulu and separately wants to reach households on Paramount+. The Hulu line is straightforward: select it as a DRAX supply source, set a budget, run the campaign. A Paramount+ line requires a different check first — confirm in your DSP console whether Paramount+ inventory is directly buyable today, or whether the Roku partnership's benefit for that household is limited to better identity matching and frequency capping on inventory you're buying elsewhere. Building a $10,000 budget on the assumption both work identically, when only one is confirmed, is how a media plan quietly underdelivers against half its named platforms.

This is exactly the kind of check worth doing before, not after, a budget commitment. A media plan document that lists six named platforms as line items looks thorough on the page; if only four of the six are genuinely selectable supply sources on the day the campaign launches, the other two either underdeliver silently or get quietly reallocated mid-flight, and either outcome undermines confidence in the rest of the plan.

What to do when a supply source you expected isn't there

If a named streaming service doesn't appear as a selectable supply source, don't assume it's a bug — check whether the relevant partnership actually grants direct-buy access or only identity/addressability benefits, since the two get conflated constantly in secondary reporting on these deals. If it's a market-availability question rather than a category question, confirm your specific market against each integration's rollout, since Disney+, ESPN, Hulu and Netflix all rolled out on different timelines and to different initial market sets before reaching broader availability.

The common mistake

The mistake is reading a press release about a streaming partnership and assuming it means the named platform is now a line item in Amazon DSP's campaign builder. Announcements about addressability, identity resolution and audience reach are real and valuable, but they answer a different question than "can I buy an ad here." reMKTR runs Amazon DSP as a managed service inside Full Circle, which has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and checks the DSP console directly for what's actually selectable before building a media plan around anything reported secondhand, however credible the source — the distance between "addressable" and "buyable" is exactly where an over-promised plan comes from.

The same discipline applies to reading this page. Every fact above is dated to when it was checked, and this category has already shown it can move quickly — Freevee disappeared as a named brand within about eighteen months of Netflix and Disney both arriving. Revisit the console, not the article, at the start of every new planning cycle.

Common questions

Can I buy Paramount+ or Tubi ads through Amazon DSP?

Check your current DSP console rather than assuming based on the Roku partnership announcement, which focused on identity resolution and addressability across Roku's device footprint rather than confirming every named publisher's inventory as a new directly-buyable Amazon DSP supply source.

Is the Roku partnership the same kind of integration as the Disney or Netflix deals?

No — Disney and Netflix both grant direct programmatic access to specific ad inventory. Roku's partnership is described as improving identity resolution and addressability across its device footprint, a related but distinct capability.

How do I check what's currently buyable without relying on news coverage?

Open your Amazon DSP console and review the available supply sources directly — that list reflects current reality, while press coverage and third-party guides can lag or generalize a partnership's actual scope.

Does 'addressable' mean the same thing as 'buyable' in Amazon's own materials?

Not necessarily, and the distinction matters for planning — addressable generally refers to identity resolution and frequency capping working consistently across a footprint, while buyable means the inventory itself is a selectable line item in the DSP.

How often does this list change?

Often enough to check before every planning cycle rather than trusting a saved list. Three major changes — Freevee's retirement, the Disney bundle, and Netflix — all landed within about eighteen months of each other, and the pace shows no sign of settling.
